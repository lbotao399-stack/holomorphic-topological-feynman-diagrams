# Local contacts and changes of first-order representative

For the heat time $t$ in the flagship propagator, let $r>0$ and $|u_1|=|u_2|=1$. Under $(\tau,z_i,t)\mapsto(r\tau,ru_i z_i,r^2t)$ the radial Gaussian is invariant. The heat kernel and the gauge-fixed integrand pull back with the respective factors

$$
[K_t]_\Omega:\ \frac1{r^2u_1u_2},\qquad
[D^*K_t]_\Omega:\ \frac1{r^4u_1u_2}.
$$

Multiplication by $dt$ restores the first factor. Dilation carries the integration interval $[\varepsilon,L]$ to $[r^2\varepsilon,r^2L]$. At fixed cutoffs, a finite graph covariance identity therefore relates two regulator values. If the tested graph distribution has a weak limit, its pullback commutes with that limit by continuity on test forms. This also applies to each of two successive limits when both exist. **Decorated graphs.** In the constant-coefficient representative $\psi_S(\phi)$ every field derivative is holomorphic. The factor $dx_{U\setminus S}$ belongs to the smooth test form of the graph. Tests can be localized near the common vertex to compute the local density. Wang and Williams prove continuous extension to compactified Schwinger space for connected graphs without self-loops. For total collapse their Proposition 3.21 gives zero for non-Laman graphs. On a Laman graph it gives a constant-coefficient holomorphic differential operator on the test form followed by restriction to the diagonal. Their Proposition 3.23 gives zero for odd loop number when a real topological direction is present. Both propositions apply to arbitrary compactly supported smooth test forms. At graph level a marked unary or binary vertex therefore requires no new valence bound. Holomorphic derivatives of a self-loop heat form vanish on the diagonal since its differential factors remain differences. [Continuous extension](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08667v3#S3.Thmthm18) · [Contact formula](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08667v3#S3.Thmthm21) · [Odd-loop vanishing](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08667v3#S3.Thmthm23)

For full-wedge holomorphic vertices the local contacts use only holomorphic field derivatives and the pulled-back exterior product at the common vertex. Positive-weight $\eta$-invariant contact terms requiring $\partial_\tau$ or $\partial_{\bar z}$ on external fields vanish at the cochain level. With one canonical $\psi_S$ at one loop, the local contact is zero. In a classical bracket of canonical representatives the constant background forms wedge, while the functional derivatives and integration by parts act on fields holomorphically. A bracket with at least one $S\ne U$ therefore has zero projection to the $S=U$ cohomology summand. Equivalently its dilation weight minus the sum of its two rotation weights is strictly negative. The inverse BV pairing adds two to the dilation weight and one to each rotation weight.

**A general exact change.** The finite-scale quantum BV identities alone do not provide the local UV primitive for a change $J_1\mapsto J_1+Q_0K$ with an arbitrary local $K$. Such a $K$ may differentiate internal kernels in the real or antiholomorphic directions. The graph theorem for holomorphic decorations does not establish finiteness of that inserted graph.

For equivalence of *existence of lifts* in the local renormalized BV theory one can use interval-valued interactions. Use the convention with the parameter differential first. Given a ghost-number $-1$ primitive $K$, the change is realized modulo $\hbar^2$ by adjoining to the original first-order lift the interval-valued term

$$
\hbar(s Q_0K+ds\,K),\qquad
(Q_0+d_s)(s Q_0K+ds\,K)=0 .
$$

The bare first-order obstruction vanishes, and the lower differential here is the full classical linearization. Costello's parametrized local obstruction is germ-local for positive field arity. Lemma 13.1.2 describes each successive extension by a closed local obstruction, and Proposition 13.2.1 proves the horn-lifting property. A lift at $s=0$ thus extends along the interval and gives a lift at $s=1$. In the translation-invariant sector the obstruction at each stage is translation invariant. The relative homotopy on an interval is integration of its $ds$ coefficient from $0$ to $s$ and commutes with spacetime translations. The resulting higher counterterm can depend on the chosen path and renormalization scheme. [Costello, Propositions 12.0.3 and 13.2.1 and Lemma 13.1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0706.1533)

Costello states this theorem on closed manifolds. For the positive-arity local flat problem, take a flat product of one real and two complex tori, use the periodic heat kernel and the same subtraction of singular coefficients. In a neighborhood of a diagonal point the periodic heat kernel is the Euclidean kernel plus its translates by nonzero periods. For a neighborhood inside the injectivity radius, the latter and each fixed derivative are bounded for small $t$ by $C t^{-N}\exp(-c/t)$ for constants $C,c>0$ and some $N$. The positive-arity obstruction is local in germs, so its short-time local coefficients and the local lifting equations coincide in these charts. Translation-invariant local densities modulo total derivatives give the same constant-coefficient jet quotient on the two spaces. On the torus one may also Haar-average a primitive at a linear lifting stage with an invariant endpoint. The order-$\hbar^2$ ghost-number-one obstruction tests positive field arity. This application concerns the local renormalized QME with local counterterms and a translation-natural scheme. It supplies homotopy invariance of the obstruction class, with no assertion of an unrenormalized UV limit for a graph marked by an arbitrary $K$.
