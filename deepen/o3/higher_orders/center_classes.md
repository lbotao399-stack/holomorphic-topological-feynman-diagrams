# Central external fields in five dimensions

Decompose each $\mathfrak{gl}_N$ input into its traceless and scalar components for the defining trace. The classical differential inserts a commutator of two traceless fields. It preserves the number of central external fields. In the stable trace algebra, let $c$ count these fields. At bidegree $(w,w)$, $w\ge2$, the dimensions in the $SL_2$-invariant subcomplex are

| $c$ | $H^3$ | $H^4$ |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 1 for $w=2$, otherwise 0 |
| 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 0 | 1 for even $w$, otherwise 0 |
| 3 | 1 for odd $w$, otherwise 0 | 0 |
| 4 | 0 | coefficient specified below |

For $c=0$ use the [traceless calculation](README.md). For $c=1$ the three- or four-cochain has a single word of traceless matrices and one fixed central momentum $q$. Its word momenta sum to $-q$. In the cyclic bar calculation, the last polygon vertex is translated after wrapping around by the sum of the sides. The ordinary bar complex in the successive independent increments still has the usual two end faces and additive interior faces: the dependent side is recovered from the fixed sum. Its polynomial cohomology consists of exterior powers on two generators tensored with the polynomial ring in $q$. The cyclic norm and duplicated-edge contraction still preserve total momentum (the extra side is zero). In exterior degrees zero and two an $SL_2$ singlet requires a constant in $q$. In exterior degree one it requires degree one in $q$. All three have total polynomial degree at most two. Taking invariants, which is exact at fixed degree, and applying the cyclic sequence proves the $c=1$ entry at $2w\ge4$.

For $c=2$ only the four-cochain with a two-letter trace survives. Write $q_1,q_2$ for central momenta, $P=-q_1-q_2$ for the traceless-pair total, and $r,P-r$ for the two traceless momenta. Closure is additivity in $r$ and vanishing at $r=P$. The proof by coprimality in the traceless calculation gives the coefficient $[rP]h(q_1,q_2)$. Invariance and homogeneity give $h$ a constant multiple of $[q_1q_2]^{w-1}$. Exchange of the central inputs requires $w$ even. The color factor is $\operatorname{tr}(X_0X_1)\operatorname{tr}(X_2)\operatorname{tr}(X_3)$ for two traceless followed by two central inputs. A three-cochain with two central fields would have an invariant traceless linear factor, which vanishes. A three-cochain with three central fields is alternating precisely for odd $w$, when its coefficient is a multiple of $[p_0p_1]^w$. Its classical differential vanishes identically.

For four central fields the four-cochains themselves are classes. The three independent momentum determinants transform under input permutations as $\Lambda^2$ of the standard three-dimensional representation of $S_4$. This is the standard representation tensored with the sign. For odd $w$ a degree-$w$ alternating coefficient is thus a symmetric polynomial on the standard representation. For even $w$ it is a Vandermonde of degree six times a symmetric polynomial of degree $w-6$: all six transposition hyperplanes divide an alternating polynomial. The symmetric polynomials in four variables summing to zero are freely generated in degrees $2,3,4$. Consequently for odd $w$ take the coefficient of $t^w$ in $1/((1-t^2)(1-t^3)(1-t^4))$; for even $w$ take its coefficient of $t^{w-6}$, zero when $w<6$. ## Consequences for the local equation

Write $j=\alpha\pi^*D_{\mathfrak{sl}}+\chi M_1+\gamma S_0$ for the manuscript's first-order term. At every even $w\ge4$, adding a multiple of $[p_0p_1]^{w-1}\operatorname{tr}(X_0)\operatorname{tr}(X_1)\operatorname{tr}(X_2)$ to the preceding cubic changes the mixed four-class. Its tree bracket with $j$, on two traceless and then two central inputs, is
$$
N\chi[p_0p_1][p_2p_3]^{w-1}\operatorname{tr}(X_0X_1)\operatorname{tr}(X_2)\operatorname{tr}(X_3).
$$
Only the shuffle placing both traceless matrices in $j$ survives, and the inverse trace pairing contracts the two central trace covectors to $N$. The one-loop local contact with the changed cubic vanishes. For $\chi\ne0$ this adjusts the entire mixed obstruction. At $w=4$ the four-central space is zero, so an order-four lift exists.

At odd $w$ each equivariant order-$k$ cubic has charge parity $(-1)^k$. With three momenta adding to zero the unique momentum polynomial is $[p_0p_1]^k$. The even-$k$ color tensor is alternating and is the commutator trace, and the odd-$k$ tensors are symmetric and all change sign under $X\mapsto-X^{\mathsf T}$. In a graph of loop number $L$ the product has charge parity $(-1)^{w-L}$. The flat heat-kernel local contact vanishes for odd $L$, including arbitrary holomorphic derivatives at vertices. Thus every odd-order obstruction is charge odd, and its invariant cohomology, entirely charge even, gives zero. These two facts give a lift through order five for every mixed first-order solution with $\chi\ne0$, in the equivariant holomorphic sector. The order-four test does constrain the previous free central cubic. ## All higher orders with a mixed cubic

Assume $\chi\ne0$, choose $\gamma=\chi/N$, and impose the manuscript's remaining two-loop equation $\alpha^2/(12N^2)-\chi^2/48=-\varepsilon c_O$. Work first in the formal trace algebra. Suppose all equations below order $w\ge3$ are satisfied. When $w$ is even, the free central cubic at order $w-1$ cancels the two-central class by the preceding formula. This changes no lower equation since its undeformed differential is zero. For odd $w$ the two-central group vanishes. The other noncentral components of the closed obstruction are exact at $w\ge3$. A choice of order-$w$ primitive therefore leaves an actual four-central cochain. Write its value as $F(p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3)\prod_i\operatorname{tr}(X_i)$, with $F$ alternating and zero total momentum.

By local BV consistency one order above the defect, $\{j,O_w\}$ is a classical boundary. Evaluate it on two copies of $X=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0,\ldots,0)$ and three central inputs $X_2,X_3,X_4$. All five matrices commute, so the boundary vanishes. The only surviving shuffle contracts the central leg of $\chi M_1$ with the four-central cochain. With $P=p_0+p_1$ one obtains
$$
0=N\chi\,[p_0p_1]\operatorname{tr}(X^2)
 F(P,p_2,p_3,p_4)\operatorname{tr}(X_2)\operatorname{tr}(X_3)\operatorname{tr}(X_4).
$$
For every $P\ne0$ one can split $P$ into two momenta with nonzero determinant. Thus $F$ is zero on a dense open subset of the momentum hyperplane and is the zero polynomial. This closes the induction. Every coefficient is fixed at the latest by the next equation, so these finite-order choices give one formal local solution. The construction uses the traceless projector, constant rational linear inverses for the classical differential and division by $N\chi$ at the mixed adjustment. In coordinates separating the center, use $\operatorname{tr}(X_i)$ and traces of projected matrices; the differential then has constant rational coefficients. Fix $\chi\ne0$ and a root of $(\alpha/N)^2=12(\chi^2/48-\varepsilon c_O)$. The resulting coefficient ring in the matrix size contains only Laurent powers of $N$. All the local identities consequently specialize to each positive integer $N$, including $N=1$ although the commuting traceless detector used in the universal proof requires $N\ge2$.

## Rank one and decoupled solutions

At $N=1$ the projected traceless cubic and $M_1$ vanish, leaving $j=\chi S_0$. Varying the nonzero $\chi$ in the previous specialization gives a rank-one lift with arbitrary nonzero first-order scalar coupling. At zero coupling the central theory is free. On a decoupled $\mathfrak{gl}_N$ branch ($\chi=0$) write a central input as $x\mathbf1_N$. The pairing is $Nxy$ and the desired first-order cubic has color coefficient $\gamma N^3$. If $S_{\mathrm{one}}$ is the rank-one action with first-order coefficient $\gamma N^3$, the central action $N S_{\mathrm{one}}(\hbar/N)$ has precisely this pairing and order-$\hbar$ term. Its local master equation follows by the same rescaling of the inverse pairing. Adding it to the independent traceless lift gives the full decoupled solution. For $N=2$ the projected symmetric traceless cubic is identically zero. Using $D=S_1-S_0$ gives $x_0D+x_1S_1=(x_0+x_1)M_1+(x_0+3x_1)S_0/2$, so the rank-two condition $x_1(x_0+x_1)=0$ is exactly $\chi(2\gamma-\chi)=0$. The unused coefficient $\alpha$ can satisfy the formal quadratic equation before specialization.

Thus every first-order solution of the manuscript's two-loop condition in this $U(2)$-equivariant, full-wedge holomorphic cubic sector admits a local QME lift to every order on $\mathbb R\times\mathbb C^2$. This is an existence statement for the smoothed local graph equations. Evaluation of a particular high-loop period or of the coefficient of its primitive is additional graph data. The first-order terms carrying explicit antiholomorphic background forms and the dictionary to untwisted five-dimensional couplings require their own calculations.

For a direct finite check confined to the rank-one sector, the cubic Poisson bracket maps the preceding three-cochains into four-cochains and then into five-cochains. Using the symmetric-polynomial basis above, exact integer minors reduced modulo $1000003$ and the Jacobi identity give zero fourth cohomology at every $2\le w\le40$ in this consistency complex.
