# Quantum supercharge corrections to determinant source wavefunctions

26 November 2026. This calculation extends the auxiliary determinant construction at the level of the quantum observable complex. It produces nonzero direct $Q_2$ and an exact all-order determinant-source response. It is **boundary-induced quantum data for the proposed giant-graviton description**, not an independent evaluation of a closed-string bulk amplitude.

The starting boundary input is the Li–Liu–Chang fan formula in its fixed renormalized operator presentation. Individual direct $Q_n$ terms can be redistributed by a change of quantum operator scheme; the complete differential and source map must be transported together. The classical determinant/brane interpretation is the one discussed by Budzik–Gaiotto, *Geometric Quantization of the Giant Graviton Master Moduli Space*, arXiv:2611.18539. Their paper explicitly defers the Yang–Mills loop corrections. Neither that source nor the calculation here establishes the full quantum holographic dictionary.

## 1. Formal auxiliary transport on the full absolute complex

For this formal construction, start with a nilpotent **full absolute** quantum Batalin–Vilkovisky differential $\mathbf Q$ on physical cochains, including the undifferentiated gauge ghost. Do not first restrict to the relative gauge-invariant complex. Let the two sets of auxiliary Grassmann variables be $\psi_i,\bar\psi_i$, with even antifields $u_i,\bar u_i$. The auxiliary odd Batalin–Vilkovisky operator is

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{aux}}=\sum_{i=1}^N
\left(\frac{\partial_L}{\partial\psi_i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_i}
+\frac{\partial_L}{\partial\bar\psi_i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar u_i}\right).
$$

Extend each coefficient $Q_n$ of this absolute differential to act on the physical factor only, leaving the auxiliary variables inert, using the graded tensor-product sign rule. This definition is meaningful for differential operators of any order; it does not extend $Q_n$ by a false Leibniz rule. Since $Q_n$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ are odd operators on separate factors,

$$
Q_n\Delta_{\mathrm{aux}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{aux}}Q_n=0,
\qquad \Delta_{\mathrm{aux}}^2=0.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\left(\sum_{n\ge0}\hbar^nQ_n+\Delta_{\mathrm{aux}}\right)^2=0.
$$

Fix the auxiliary integration order by

$$
\int d^N\bar\psi\,d^N\psi\,
\exp(\bar\psi M\psi)=\det M.
$$

The measure has even parity. Berezin integration annihilates every $\partial_{\psi_i}$ or $\partial_{\bar\psi_i}$ total derivative, including coefficients polynomial in the auxiliary antifields. Hence

$$
\left.\int d^N\bar\psi\,d^N\psi\,
\left(\mathbf Q+\Delta_{\mathrm{aux}}\right)f\right|_{u=\bar u=0}
=\mathbf Q\left.\int d^N\bar\psi\,d^N\psi\,f\right|_{u=\bar u=0}.
$$

Auxiliary antifields are set to zero after integration, so the target is the physical absolute cochain complex. This is a formal exact identity for a supplied absolute differential; higher-order $Q_n$ do not spoil it.

For $f=e^S$ and the matrix sources used below, the integrated determinant is gauge invariant and independent of the undifferentiated ghost, so its quantum variation can be evaluated in the paper's relative complex. This does **not** make the intermediate auxiliary complex a joint relative gauge complex. If one instead makes the auxiliary variables transform as gauge fundamentals before integration, extra gauge terms are needed; the higher $Q_n$ can contract the ghost in those terms and produce further cross-corrections. The explicit first cross-contraction and required correction equation are derived in [AUXILIARY_GAUGE_TERM.md](AUXILIARY_GAUGE_TERM.md). No naive relative restriction, uniform-rank trace/meson resolution, or quantum quasi-isomorphism to the Budzik–Gaiotto brane complex is asserted here.

## 2. The source-conjugated differential contains new contact operations

Let $S=\bar\psi M(C)\psi$, with $M(C)$ an even matrix-valued polynomial in the physical fields and external graded sources. Multiplication by $e^S$ is invertible as a finite auxiliary Grassmann series. Set

$$
D_S=e^{-S}(\mathbf Q+\Delta_{\mathrm{aux}})e^S.
$$

It follows directly that $D_S^2=0$, and the weighted integral satisfies

$$
\left.\int e^S D_S f\right|_{u=\bar u=0}=\mathbf Q\left.\int e^S f\right|_{u=\bar u=0}.
$$

All integrations in this equation are over the auxiliary variables, followed by evaluation at zero auxiliary antifields. Since $Q_n$ has differential order $n+1$ on the physical fields,

$$
\boxed{
 e^{-S}Q_ne^S
 =\sum_{r=0}^{n+1}\frac1{r!}
 [\cdots[[Q_n,S],S],\ldots,S],
}
$$

where the term $r=0$ is $Q_n$ and each $S$ denotes its multiplication operator. There are no terms with $r>n+1$. As $S$ contains no auxiliary antifields, $\Delta_{\mathrm{aux}}S=0$ and its self-antibracket vanishes. The auxiliary conjugation therefore adds only the Hamiltonian first-order operator generated by $S$.

At order one the new physical-field part is

$$
[Q_1,S]+\frac12[[Q_1,S],S].
$$

At order two it is

$$
[Q_2,S]+\frac12[[Q_2,S],S]+\frac16[[[Q_2,S],S],S].
$$

The double and triple commutators are the cross-contractions missed by treating the quantum charge as a derivation on trace/meson variables. All of these terms are fixed before passing to cohomology. Their calculation is an algebraic transport of the boundary operator, so it is a prediction for the brane source complex rather than an independent holography check.


Nilpotent conjugation does not imply closure of the source:

$$
D_S1=e^{-S}\mathbf Q(e^S)
$$

is generally nonzero. This construction neither solves a brane quantum master equation nor proves a BPS brane-state deformation. A classical example already shows the issue. In the paper's superfield convention, at zero undifferentiated ghost,

$$
Q_0\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(m\mathbf1+\gamma^1)\beta_1\bigr)
=-\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(m\mathbf1+\gamma^1)[\gamma^2,\gamma^3]\bigr).
$$

At rank two, take $\gamma^1=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, $\gamma^2=E_{12}$ and $\gamma^3=E_{21}$. The last expression equals $2$. The quantum coefficients computed below are therefore **off-shell source Ward/contact terms**; they are not an energy shift or a proved deformation of a protected brane state.

## 3. A determinant family detecting each direct Q_n

Use the paper's matrix fields, coupling $g$, trace convention

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(t_A t_B)=\tau\delta_{AB},\qquad
[t_A,t_B]=iC_{AB}{}^Ct_C.
$$

Take three odd external source variables $\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_3$ and one even variable $v$. They are independent of the physical fields and are annihilated by $\mathbf Q$. The determinant source is

$$
\det\bigl(m\mathbf1+\gamma^1+\eta_1\beta_1+\eta_2\beta_2+\eta_3\beta_3+v b\bigr).
$$

The products $\eta_I\beta_I$ are even. At $\beta=b=0$ take $m\mathbf1+\gamma^1=\operatorname{diag}(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_N)$, with $m=N^{-1}\sum_i\mu_i$ so that $\gamma^1$ is traceless. Set the other two scalars to zero. The two ghost waves have ordered odd amplitudes $\epsilon_+,\epsilon_-$, colors $E_{ab},E_{ba}$ with $a\ne b$, and derivative eigenvalues $p_{+\dot a},q_{+\dot a}$. Explicitly,

$$
c(x)=\epsilon_+E_{ab}(e^{-p_{+\dot a}x^{+\dot a}/2}-1)
+\epsilon_-E_{ba}(e^{-q_{+\dot a}x^{+\dot a}/2}-1).
$$

This uses exactly the paper's spinor-derivative convention. The undifferentiated ghost vanishes at the insertion.

For $n\ge2$, extract the ordered coefficient of
$\eta_1\eta_2\eta_3 v^{n-2}\epsilon_+\epsilon_-$ in the full quantum variation. Only $Q_n$ contributes. The input contains three beta letters and $n-2$ copies of $b$ that must all be differentiated. An additional scalar derivative would raise the common Berezin degree from three to five. Thus the complete fan has exactly $n+1$ spokes.

To state the exact color polynomial, let $e_j$ be the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree $j$ in the $N-2$ diagonal entries with indices other than $a,b$. The conventions are $e_0=1$ and $e_j=0$ outside $0\le j\le N-2$.

The matrix-unit color contraction with $k$ spokes is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C_k={}&(k-1)!\left((-1)^{k-1}\mu_b-\mu_a\right)e_{N-k-1}\\
&-\left(1+(-1)^k\right)k(k-2)!e_{N-k}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

The complete measured determinant variation is

$$
\boxed{
-\frac{\hbar^n(ig\tau)^n}{(32\pi^2)^n(n!)^2(n-2)!}
\left(p_{+\dot a}q_+{}^{\dot a}\right)^n\mathcal C_{n+1},
\qquad n\ge2.
}
$$

This formula is polynomial in all $\mu_i$, including singular $M$; no inverse matrix assumption is required.

### Source signs and the factorial

Let $H_k$ denote the symmetric $k$th derivative of the determinant with respect to its even matrix argument. The ordered beta polynomial is

$$
H_{n+1}(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3,b,\ldots,b).
$$

Its coefficient in the determinant is

$$
[\eta_1\eta_2\eta_3v^{n-2}]\det
=-\frac1{(n-2)!}
H_{n+1}(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3,b,\ldots,b).
$$

The minus sign comes from moving the three odd beta letters past the odd sources. Applying the odd $\mathbf Q$ produces a second minus when it passes $\eta_1\eta_2\eta_3$. Thus the coefficient in $\mathbf Q\det$ is plus the variation of this polynomial divided by $(n-2)!$.

All $(n+1)!$ labeled spoke assignments have the same Berezin sign. With the original fan factor $1/(2(32\pi^2)^n(n!)^3(n+1))$, the two endpoint assignments cancel $1/2$ and the assignment count gives $1/(n!)^2$. The three beta contractions, $n-2$ $b$ contractions, two ghost endpoints, and conversion of the color chain give phase $-(ig\tau)^n$, exactly as in the existing all-order fan convention.

## 4. Proof of the color polynomial by determinant cofactors

For a diagonal matrix, its $k$th derivative on matrix units vanishes unless the $k$ selected rows and columns are distinct and form the same subset. For a row subset of size $k$, a permutation of its columns contributes its permutation sign times the product of all unselected diagonal entries. Contract each unit against its trace-dual unit in the nested adjoint chain acting on $E_{ab}$, and then take the $E_{ba}$ trace.

The exact subset weights are

| Root-index membership in the selected subset | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Neither $a$ nor $b$ | $0$ |
| $a$ only | $(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!$ |
| $b$ only | $-(k-1)!$ |
| Both | $-[1+(-1)^k]k(k-2)!$ |

For an $a$-only subset, every commutator must act by left multiplication. The first row must be $a$; the remaining $(k-1)!$ row orders each give one $k$-cycle, of sign $(-1)^{k-1}$. For a $b$-only subset, all multiplications are on the right, the last row must be $b$, and the right-action signs give $-(k-1)!$.

If both endpoints occur, the two unmixed contributions sum to

$$
-[1+(-1)^k](k-1)!.
$$

For a mixed contribution with $\ell$ left multiplications, choose their positions in $\binom{k}{\ell}$ ways. The first left row is $a$ and the last right row is $b$; the remaining rows have $(k-2)!$ orders. The two resulting cycles and the right-action signs give $(-1)^\ell$. Therefore all mixed contributions sum to

$$
(k-2)!\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(-1)^\ell\binom{k}{\ell}
=-[1+(-1)^k](k-2)!.
$$

Adding the unmixed terms gives the last table entry. Summing the unselected diagonal products for the three nonzero membership cases gives the boxed $\mathcal C_k$. Fundamental completeness contributes $\tau^k$; conversion of the adjoint-chain endpoint trace removes one $\tau$. The central term in $SU(N)$ completeness vanishes inside every commutator, leaving $\tau^{k-1}$ as required.

A second unrestricted proof, using Grassmann matrices instead of cofactors, appears in [GRASSMANN_PROOF.md](GRASSMANN_PROOF.md). Its verifier imports neither this cofactor evaluator nor the boundary graph engine.

## 5. Direct Q2 and Q3 examples

At $\tau=1/2$ and $p_{+\dot a}q_+{}^{\dot a}=1$,

$$
\mathcal C_3=2(\mu_b-\mu_a)e_{N-4},
$$

so the ordered $\eta_1\eta_2\eta_3\epsilon_+\epsilon_-$ coefficient is

$$
\boxed{\frac{\hbar^2g^2}{8(32\pi^2)^2}
(\mu_b-\mu_a)e_{N-4}.}
$$

At rank four with diagonal $(3,5,7,9)$ and root $(a,b)=(1,2)$, this equals

$$
\boxed{\frac{\hbar^2g^2}{4(32\pi^2)^2}
=\frac{\hbar^2g^2}{4096\pi^4}.}
$$

This coefficient is a direct $Q_2$ effect: all other $Q_k$ vanish in the same specified measurement. It is not a spectral-sequence $d_2$ or a lifting assertion.

For $n=3$ at the same rank, $\mathcal C_4=-16$. The ordered coefficient with one additional $v$ is

$$
\boxed{-\frac{i\hbar^3g^3}{18(32\pi^2)^3}
=-\frac{i\hbar^3g^3}{589824\pi^6}.}
$$

The rank thresholds follow from the exact elementary symmetric polynomials:

- Even $n\ge2$: the first possible nonzero single-determinant response has $N=n+2$, with $\mu_a\ne\mu_b$.
- Odd $n\ge3$: it already occurs at $N=n+1$; at $M=m\mathbf1$,
  $\mathcal C_{n+1}=-2N(N-2)!m^{N-n-1}/(N-n-1)!$.

These are statements about this chosen single-determinant family. They do not assert that $Q_n$ vanishes on every operator below these ranks.

## 6. The one-loop determinant deformation

For the odd operator $\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{adj}(M)\beta_1)$, the same two-root measurement gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{\hbar g^2\tau}{32\pi^2}\mathcal C_2.}
$$

For nonsingular diagonal $M$, write $r_i=\mu_i^{-1}$. Direct differentiation of the determinant also gives

$$
\mathcal C_2=-\det M\left[(r_a+r_b)\sum_i r_i-(r_a-r_b)^2\right].
$$

The polynomial cofactor expression proves extension across $\det M=0$. At $\tau=1/2$ and $M=m\mathbf1$ the answer is

$$
-\frac{\hbar g^2N}{32\pi^2}m^{N-2}.
$$

At $N=2$, $\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{adj}(M)\beta_1)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^1\beta_1)$, which checks the known one-loop normalization independently. For the source convention $M+\eta_1\beta_1$, the literal $\eta_1$ coefficient of $\mathbf Q\det$ has the opposite sign because $\mathbf Q$ passes one odd source.
