# Orthogonal Pfaffians from the dressed binary bracket

For $\mathfrak{so}_{2N}$ every zero-holomorphic-derivative class and every maximal-relative-length class has a finite quantum lift. The two statements concern respectively the relative Cartan algebra of $x,y,b$ and that of $x,y,\partial_1c,\partial_2c,\partial_3c$. Their classical identification uses the derived commuting-pair and multilinear orthogonal restriction theorems. ## Two scalar flavors

For two closed operators use the interaction-dressed binary bracket at zero external bracket parameter. The two-insertion quadratic Ward identity makes this bracket a closed operator. At loop order $n$ it has $2n$ cubic vertices and $3n+1$ physical propagators: the three-complex-dimensional tightness condition and the connected loop count give these numbers. Thus it lowers both odd-coordinate weights by $n+1$, and the sum terminates on polynomial inputs. At order zero the single physical edge directly pairs the two inputs. The $x,y$ derivatives give $\theta_2(-\theta_1)=\theta_1\theta_2$, with the opposite sign in the reversed order. The one-edge Schwinger integral has no loop variables and the normalized collision heat kernel evaluates to the identity. The resulting scalar operation is the canonical contraction of the $x$ derivative of the first input with the $y$ derivative of the second by the inverse action pairing, minus the exchanged flavors, with the overall binary-bracket convention. [Dressed bracket and its Ward identity](../../../sources/LLC_main.tex)

Use the defining trace pairing and Cartan eigenvalues $\pm x_i,\pm y_i$. On regular diagonal pairs the gradients of an invariant scalar polynomial lie in the Cartan by torus invariance. The defining trace counts each Cartan eigenvalue pair twice, which cancels the factor $1/2$ in the inverse Cartan pairing. For odd $k\ge1$, the ordered tree bracket with $\operatorname{tr}(y^{k+1})/(k+1)$ on the right acts on the Cartan restriction $F$ by
$$
\sum_{\substack{1\le i\le N\\\mathrm{Cartan\ eigenvalue\ pairs}}}
y_i^k\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_i}.
$$
A different scalar multiple of the action pairing multiplies this operation by its inverse. Reversing the binary edge order reverses the scalar bracket. In every convention the dressed bracket supplies a closed lift of the displayed polynomial after multiplication by the corresponding nonzero scalar.

Hunziker's Theorem 3.1 gives generators of the two-copy $D_N$ Weyl-invariant algebra: ordinary polarizations of its basic invariants and successive applications of the displayed operators to the fundamental Pfaffian. Ordinary polarizations of a single invariant are strict trace or invariant-tensor descendants; the identical-odd-factor proof applies to the symmetric Pfaffian tensor. Use successive dressed brackets for the extra generators and marked products for multiplication. The result gives all scalar classes. In $D_4$ the first additional example is
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{4}y_i^3\prod_{j\ne i}x_j
=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\prod_{i=1}^{4}(x_i+t y_i^3)\right|_{t=0}.
$$
Its values increase the exact rank of the ordinary polarized-generator products in bidegree $(3,3)$ on 34 integer test pairs from nine to ten. [Hunziker, Theorem 3.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01235938) · [Exact replay](type_d.py)

## Polarization into odd slots

Hunziker's Theorem 3.4 states that for any number of ordinary vector flavors the type-$D_N$ Weyl invariants are generated by polarizations of the full two-copy algebra. Apply it in the multilinear slots of the additional flavors, and project with the signed permutation action for each repeated odd flavor. This produces the graded invariant algebra from products of polarized two-copy functions. To implement the projection on one scalar generator, substitute odd scalar parameters times the desired odd vectors into its even arguments and extract the ordered multilinear parameter coefficient. [Hunziker, Theorem 3.4](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01235938)

For a $b$ slot the substitutions $x\mapsto x+\varepsilon_2b$, $y\mapsto y-\varepsilon_1b$ are whole-superfield odd translations, up to the order chosen for the odd parameters. They commute with every graph differential since edge kernels depend on differences and the Berezin measures are translation invariant. For a slot $\partial_i c$, use the source shear
$$
z^i\longmapsto z^i+\varepsilon\theta_j .
$$
Its Berezinian is one. On an edge between source points its change of even displacement is $\varepsilon(\theta_{vj}-\theta_{wj})$. The edge carries the full factor $(\theta_{v1}-\theta_{w1})(\theta_{v2}-\theta_{w2})$, so the change in the heat kernel or propagator vanishes identically. This argument precedes taking any field jets and applies at each integration scale. Its odd generator on the superfield is $\theta_j\partial_iC$. In left-derivative conventions it acts on $g_j$ by $-\partial_i c$; on $b$ its action is $\partial_i y$ for $j=1$ and $-\partial_i x$ for $j=2$. Each desired shift of a scalar into a positive ghost mode is therefore realized on the closed operator before extracting its component.

Ordinary linear substitutions of $x,y$ in these polarizations can also be implemented on a lift. A determinant-one change of the two odd coordinates is a graph symmetry. Scalar rescalings act term by term in homogeneous field length on the leading representative; equivalently combine a scalar multiple of the whole leading class with a determinant-one change. Singular linear substitutions are coefficients or linear combinations of values at nonsingular matrices by polynomial interpolation. These transformations, followed by odd translations or shears of the chosen directions, realize every polarization of the two-copy generators, and preserve finite perturbative closure. There are no incoming pages at zero holomorphic order. At maximal length the only possible incoming page originates on the same face at $r=1$, where all classes now have closed lifts. Thus all these relative classes and their absolute constant-ghost partners survive.
