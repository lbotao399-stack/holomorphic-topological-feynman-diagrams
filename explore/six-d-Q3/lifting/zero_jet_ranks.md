# Zero holomorphic derivatives at finite matrix rank

For $\mathfrak{sl}_N$ at every matrix rank $N\ge2$, the entire local quantum spectral sequence at zero holomorphic derivative degree consists of the diagonal scalar and antifield polynomials invariant under simultaneous permutations, tensored with the ordinary constant-ghost cohomology. On the Cartan $\mathfrak h$ the two scalar slots $x,y$ are even and the single antifield slot $b$ is odd. Write $l$ for field length and $u,v$ for the two odd-coordinate weights. The absolute dimension series is
$$
\frac{\prod_{j=2}^{N}(1+l^{2j-1})}{N!}
\sum_{\substack{w\in S_N\\\mathrm{permutations}}}
\frac{\det_{\mathfrak h}(1+luv\,w)}
{\det_{\mathfrak h}(1-lu\,w)\det_{\mathfrak h}(1-lv\,w)} .
$$
The elementary Koszul calculation below proves the result through $N=4$. The derived commuting-stack calculation at the end proves it for every rank, gives the analogous complete quantum result in types $B,C$, and computes the classical zero-jet sector for every complex reductive gauge algebra.

## Upper Koszul homology

Set $S=\mathbb C[\mathfrak g\oplus\mathfrak g]$, with $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_N$, $m=\dim\mathfrak g$, $r=\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak g$, and let $I$ be the ideal generated by the $m$ components of $[x,y]$. Write $K$ for their Koszul complex; its homological degree is the number of $b$ fields in a relative cochain. Multiplication by an element of $I$ is nullhomotopic on $K$. Richardson's theorem identifies the reduced commuting variety as the closure of conjugates of Cartan pairs. Its regular semisimple open has dimension $m+r$, by choosing regular $x$ followed by $y$ in its $r$-dimensional centralizer. Hence $P=\sqrt I$ is prime and has height $c=m-r$. On this open the root equations solve for the off-diagonal components of $y$, so $S/I$ is generically reduced. In particular $P/I$ has codimension at least $c+1$. [Richardson, Theorem A](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1979__38_3_311_0/) · [Regular slice](one_jet/regular.md)

For any finite module $M$ supported in codimension at least $d$, $\operatorname{Ext}^i_S(M,S)=0$ for $i<d$, since $S$ is regular and grade equals codimension. Apply the hypercohomology spectral sequence to $\operatorname{Hom}_S(K,S)$:
$$
E_2^{i,j}=\operatorname{Ext}^i_S(H_j(K),S)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_{m-i-j}(K)
$$
up to the common shift of internal weights from Koszul self-duality. Only columns $i\ge c$ can occur. This yields $H_j(K)=0$ for $j>r$ and
$$
H_r(K)=\operatorname{Ext}^c_S(S/I,S)
      =\operatorname{Ext}^c_S(S/P,S)
$$
with that shift. The second equality follows from the codimension of $P/I$. For $f\notin P$, the exact sequence defined by multiplication by $f$ on $S/P$ has cokernel supported in codimension at least $c+1$. Its Ext sequence shows that multiplication by $f$ is injective on the displayed canonical module. Thus the highest Koszul homology injects upon localization at the regular discriminant, including after color invariants.

In the four-by-four case the commuting matrix ring is Cohen–Macaulay over characteristic zero. Removing the two independent scalar trace coordinates preserves that property for traceless pairs. In particular $\operatorname{Ext}^{c+1}_S(S/I,S)=0$. In total degree $c+1$ the only remaining term is a submodule of $\operatorname{Ext}^c_S(H_1(K),S)$, since no term can map into its column from the left. Thus $H_{r-1}(K)$ also injects into its localization at the regular discriminant. Explicitly, for any module $M$ supported on $V(P)$ and any $f\notin P$, both $\ker(f:M\to M)$ and $M/fM$ have support of codimension at least $c+1$. Applying Ext to $M\to fM$ and $fM\hookrightarrow M$ shows that multiplication by $f$ is injective on $\operatorname{Ext}^c_S(M,S)$. [Hreinsdóttir, four-by-four Cohen–Macaulay calculation](https://doi.org/10.1006/jsco.1994.1053)

## Cartan image and Euler characteristic

On the diagonal let $a_i,d_i,\eta_i$ be the entries of $x,y,b$, with $\eta_i$ odd. The power sums of the monomials $a_i^j d_i^k$ and $a_i^j d_i^k\eta_i$ generate all signed permutation invariants by distinct-label inclusion–exclusion. Taking traceless matrices removes just the three linear sums. The scalar generators are obtained from $\operatorname{tr}((x+t y)^{j+k})$. The single-antifield generators are obtained by extracting the $t^k$ coefficient from $\operatorname{tr}((x+t y)^{j+k}b)$, which is itself a coefficient of a strict trace descendant: differentiate $\operatorname{tr}(((\partial_{\theta_1}+t\partial_{\theta_2})C)^{j+k+1})$ in $\theta_2$ and divide by $j+k+1$. Thus restriction is surjective at each antifield degree and bidegree, and every diagonal class has a finite marked lift. Degree zero is injective by the two-even-flavor commuting-matrix trace relations. At degree $r$ localization injectivity just proved gives injectivity into diagonal data. For $N=4$ it does the same at degree $r-1$. [Trace relations and distinct-label sums](relative.md) · [Strict trace descendants](single_trace.md)

The bigraded Euler identity of Berest–Felder–Patotski–Ramadoss–Willwacher states that the adjoint Koszul complex and diagonal Weyl algebra have equal signed Euler series, with sign $(-1)^{\# b}$. For traceless matrices this follows by removing the same free scalar factor on both sides; its series has constant term one. For $N=3$, only degrees $0,1,2$ occur and the restrictions in degrees zero and two are isomorphisms. For $N=4$, only degrees $0,1,2,3$ occur and the restrictions in degrees zero, two and three are isomorphisms. In each case the Euler identity equates the remaining degree-one dimensions coefficientwise. Surjectivity then makes the remaining restriction an isomorphism. This proves the claimed complete relative answer. [Euler identity, Theorem 5.5](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/729) · [Elementary rank-two matrix case](sl2_zero_jets.md)

The constant-color factor gives the absolute series. All listed cohomology classes have finite marked lifts, and a positive-page incoming differential would have to start at negative holomorphic derivative degree. Every class survives. The replay extracts singlets from the adjoint Koszul Euler character with the Weyl denominator, independently computes the finite permutation-character sum on the Cartan, and compares 77 bidegrees across $N=2,3,4$. ## All ranks by completion of the derived commuting stack

For a complex reductive Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ with Cartan $\mathfrak h$ and Weyl group $W$, actual restriction induces, in every antifield degree $j$,
$$
H_j(K)^G\ \longrightarrow\ 
\big(\mathbb C[\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak h]\otimes\Lambda^j\mathfrak h^*\big)^W
$$
as an isomorphism of modules over the scalar commuting-pair ring. Choose a connected group $G$ with this Lie algebra and simply connected derived subgroup, which is possible without changing the adjoint action. The following argument uses one multiplicative derived-stack theorem and proves the additive comparison.

**Multiplicative input.** Li–Nadler–Yun define the derived stack $Z^2_G$ as the adjoint quotient of the derived fiber over the identity of the group commutator $G^2\to G$. Their Theorem 1.4.7 and Remark 1.4.8 give an equivalence of its dg algebra of global functions with $(\mathcal O(T^2)\otimes\Lambda^\bullet\mathfrak h^*)^W$, where $T$ is a maximal torus and the exterior generators have homological degree one. In their proof $\mathcal O(Z^2_G)$ is the derived endomorphism algebra of the structure sheaf. Simple connectedness of the derived subgroup selects the single torus term. Their Theorem 1.4.11 also identifies the *actual restriction* in additive degree zero with $\mathbb C[\mathfrak h^2]^W$, including scheme structure. [Li–Nadler–Yun, Annals of Mathematics (2024), §§1.4 and 6.3–6.4](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2024.200.2.5)

The universal Hecke input stated as Ansatz 1.4.2 there is supplied by the Dhillon–Taylor universal monodromic equivalence. Their Theorem 1.1 compares the full *multiplicative* Steinberg category with the universal affine Hecke category and identifies Iwahori–Whittaker averaging with direct images along the projections. Passing to adjoints identifies their comonads and the corresponding coalgebra kernels. In the complex-coefficient construction the Whittaker adjunction realizes comodules for the big tilting coalgebra, and the Steinberg structure sheaf is taken to this object (v1, §§2.3, 3.2). The coproduct on the spectral structure sheaf is the adjunction unit in Li–Nadler–Yun §2.6.6; their Remark 1.4.3 states that the additional bimodule clause of the Ansatz is unused. The torus-monodromy specialization enters only after the universal equivalence. [Dhillon–Taylor, Theorem 1.1, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14157v2) · [Complex-coefficient Whittaker compatibility, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14157v1)

In particular each multiplicative homology module over multiplicative $H_0$ is reflexive, meaning that the natural map to its double dual is an isomorphism. Indeed a module $(\mathcal O(T^2)\otimes V)^W$ for a finite-dimensional $W$-representation $V$ is torsion-free and determined inside its generic fiber by regularity at codimension-one points. The finite torus quotient sends each height-one prime to height one. Pulling a rational section to $T^2$ then uses ordinary regularity in codimension one on a smooth variety, and equivariance descends it. The dg ring equivalence preserves this module property regardless of the choice of isomorphism on $H_0$.

**Formal comparison.** Let $\mathfrak m$ be the maximal ideal of the fixed point $(1,1)$ of $G^2$. For a finite equivariant module $M$ over the ambient coordinate ring $A$, the invariants of its $\mathfrak m$-adic completion are the completion of $M^G$ at $\mathfrak n=\mathfrak m\cap A^G$. In fact the Rees module $\bigoplus_{k\ge0}\mathfrak m^k M\,t^k$ is finite over the Rees algebra of $\mathfrak m$, whose generators are finite in number. Reductivity and invariant finite generation make the invariant Rees module finite over the invariant Rees algebra. Each coefficient of a positive-degree algebra generator belongs to $\mathfrak n$. With $D$ bounding their degrees and $D'$ the module-generator degrees, every invariant coefficient in filtration degree $k>D'$ belongs to $\mathfrak n^{\lceil(k-D')/D\rceil}M^G$. The reverse containment at degree $k$ follows by multiplying $k$ invariant elements of $\mathfrak m$. Exactness of taking rational invariants and passage to the inverse limit now give the desired equality of completions. This also applies at the origin of $\mathfrak g^2$.

The identity point in $G$ is a regular embedding; the derived fiber has a bounded complex with coherent homology, computed on the formal neighborhood by its Koszul complex in logarithmic coordinates. Flat completion therefore commutes with homology. Write the formal group coordinates as $\exp x,\exp y$. The logarithm of their group commutator equals $U(x,y)[x,y]$, where $U=1+$ terms of positive degree in $\operatorname{ad}_x,\operatorname{ad}_y$. Indeed its leading term is $[x,y]$; all higher terms are Lie words of degree at least three. The ideal of Lie words generated by $[x,y]$ is spanned by its successive adjoints by $x,y$, by Jacobi. The operator $U$ is equivariant and formally invertible. Changing the odd Koszul generators by $U$ therefore identifies the completed multiplicative complex and the completed additive complex, compatibly with constant colors. This step compares the derived fibers themselves.

Finite reflexive modules remain reflexive under flat completion since finite presentation allows both Hom-duals to commute with this base change. Thus the completed additive modules are reflexive over completed additive $H_0$. Their uncompleted counterparts are finite graded modules for the positive grading assigning degrees one to $x,y$ and two to $b$. For the double-dual map, both its kernel and cokernel are finite graded modules whose completions at the homogeneous origin vanish. Graded Nakayama forces both to vanish. The actual additive homology modules are therefore reflexive.

**Restriction and quantum representatives.** On each open where a scalar polarization $x+t y$ is regular semisimple the root Koszul pairs contract. Consequently actual restriction to the Cartan is an isomorphism on those opens. In $\mathfrak h^2$ the complement of their union consists of the subspaces $\alpha(x)=\alpha(y)=0$ for roots $\alpha$: the polynomial $\prod_{\alpha}\alpha(x+t y)$ is identically zero in $t$ precisely when one such root vanishes identically. Each subspace has codimension two and its finite Weyl quotient has the same codimension. Over the normal base $\mathfrak h^2/W$, both the source and target of the displayed restriction are reflexive. They agree in codimension one, hence globally by intersection inside the generic fiber. This proves the classical result for all reductive colors without having to assume that a categorical equivalence itself realizes restriction on higher homology. [Root contraction on every regular polarization](one_jet/regular.md)

In type $A$, each Cartan invariant has a finite lift by the scalar and single-$b$ power sums above. In types $B,C$, sign reversal at each eigenvalue requires the monomial in its two scalar entries and one odd antifield to have even total length. Permutation invariance is generated by the power sums of these even-length monomials, by the same distinct-label expansion. In the defining representation their traces on the Cartan are twice the power sums, pairing the opposite eigenvalues. Hence the strict descendants and their marked products lift every class here also. At zero holomorphic order there are no incoming quantum differentials. Thus for $A,B,C$ the displayed Cartan algebra, with the ordinary constant-ghost factor, is the complete quantum answer at every rank. The preceding $S_N$ dimension formula applies to every $N$ in type $A$. For $B,C$ use the signed-permutation Weyl group on the rank-$r$ Cartan, and the constant-color factor $\prod_{j=1}^r(1+l^{4j-1})$. The next calculation covers $G_2$. The dressed binary bracket provides the additional type-$D$ Pfaffian lifts. [Orthogonal calculation](type_d.md) For the remaining exceptional groups the reflexive-module argument gives the classical answer.

## The exceptional algebra $\mathfrak g_2$

Here the Weyl group is dihedral of order twelve. In a complex Cartan basis its rotation scales the two coordinates of any flavor $i$ as $a_i^+\mapsto\zeta a_i^+$ and $a_i^-\mapsto\zeta^{-1}a_i^-$ for a primitive sixth root $\zeta$. Reflection interchanges the signs. In ordinary multilinear variables the polarized Chevalley generators can be chosen as
$$
Q_{ij}=a_i^+a_j^-+a_i^-a_j^+,\qquad
P_I=\prod_{i\in I}a_i^++\prod_{i\in I}a_i^-\qquad(|I|=6).
$$
A rotation-invariant monomial splits into opposite-sign pairs and pure sextuples. Each block is a sum of its reflection-even and reflection-odd parts. Write $R_{ij}=a_i^+a_j^--a_i^-a_j^+$ and $S_I=\prod_{i\in I}a_i^+-\prod_{i\in I}a_i^-$. Reflection-invariant expressions contain an even number of these odd blocks. Two $R$ factors reduce by the two-dimensional Gram determinant identity. The product $S_I S_J$ equals $P_I P_J$ minus twice the sum of the two mixed-sign monomials; this sum is invariant under the full orthogonal group since it has balanced numbers of plus and minus slots and is invariant under reflection. Orthogonal pair contractions express it in the $Q_{ij}$. For $I=\{k\}\cup J$, direct expansion gives
$$
R_{ij} S_I=Q_{jk}P_{\{i\}\cup J}-Q_{ik}P_{\{j\}\cup J}.
$$
These relations eliminate all pairs of reflection-odd blocks. Projecting the multilinear identity to the two even scalar flavors and one odd antifield flavor supplies all Koszul signs. Every surviving polarized Chevalley generator contains at most one occurrence of $b$, since its color tensor is symmetric. With no $b$ it is a scalar coefficient of the invariant applied to $x+t y$; with one $b$ it is obtained by the whole-$\theta_2$ derivative of the polarized strict descendant used above. Their marked products therefore give finite lifts of the complete zero-holomorphic sector for $\mathfrak g_2$. Its constant-ghost factor is $(1+l^3)(1+l^{11})$.
