# Constant ghosts and the sector of maximal field length

## Absolute and relative quantum cohomology

The local relative complex consists of constant-color invariant cochains independent of the undifferentiated ghost $c(0)$. At tree level the terms containing $c(0)$ in the variation of such a cochain are its simultaneous infinitesimal color rotation and cancel. At every positive loop it remains a relative cochain graph by graph.

Indeed the sparsity bound forbids an interaction vertex of valence one: deleting such a vertex in a tight graph removes one edge and violates the bound on the remainder. Each cubic interaction that bears an output therefore has degree two and carries precisely one surviving field. At an output vertex with external momentum $\lambda_v=0$, Kirchhoff conservation equates the two incident currents up to their orientations. The currents everywhere depend on the two incident edge resistances $t_e,t_f$ only through $t_e+t_f$, by the series law. Thus the tangent variation
$$
(t_e,t_f)\longmapsto(t_e+s,t_f-s)
$$
preserves the normalized Schwinger simplex and all cotree currents. Their top exterior differential vanishes pointwise, also after multiplying by any polynomial in the currents from root displacements. The resulting polynomial kernel has no term independent of $\lambda_v$. Hence every output carries at least one holomorphic derivative. In particular $Q_{n\ge1}$ cannot produce $c(0)$ from relative inputs. Color invariance follows from contractions of invariant tensors. [Kirchhoff kernel and sparsity](../VERIFY.md)

For reductive constant colors $\mathfrak g$, choose homogeneous polynomial representatives in $c(0)$ for a vector-space basis of $H^*(\mathfrak g,\mathbb C)$. Each is quantum closed since two ghost derivatives at the common odd point multiply $(\theta_1\theta_2)^2=0$. For such a representative $P$ and a relative cochain $F$, the marked identity gives
$$
\mathbf Q(P\star F)=(-1)^{|P|}P\star\mathbf QF .
$$
The resulting linear map from $H^*(\mathfrak g,\mathbb C)\otimes C_{\rm rel}[[\hbar]]$ to absolute cochains preserves the filtration. At $\hbar=0$ it is the ordinary multiplication map on cohomology. To see that this is an isomorphism, split off constant colors as a Lie subalgebra from the ideal of positive source modes. Resolve this ideal first. At every multidegree the complex of its cochains splits equivariantly into homology and contractible pairs by complete reducibility. Lie algebra cohomology with such coefficients equals $H^*(\mathfrak g,\mathbb C)$ tensored with the invariant summands, by the compact-form Casimir homotopy on the nontrivial irreducibles. This gives the relative answer on those summands. The filtered comparison gives, additively at every page,
$$
E_r^{\rm abs}=H^*(\mathfrak g,\mathbb C)\otimes E_r^{\rm rel},\qquad r\ge1 .
$$
The graph argument concerns nonintegrated local operators. [Marked chain identity](scalar_products/README.md)

## Maximal relative length for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$

For a relative cochain write $p$ for its field length and $|\mu|$ for the sum of all three holomorphic and both odd coordinate weights. Every relative letter has positive weight, hence $p\le|\mu|$. At $p=|\mu|$ only $x=g_1,y=g_2$ and the three odd letters $\partial_i c$ occur. They are classically closed up to simultaneous constant-color conjugation. A boundary comes from a single letter of weight two with first modes as spectators. The possible degree-two source monomials and their brackets are
$$
\theta_1\theta_2:\ [x,y],\qquad
z^i\theta_1:\ [\partial_i c,x],\qquad
z^i\theta_2:\ [\partial_i c,y],\qquad
z^i z^j:\ [\partial_i c,\partial_j c].
$$
The last entries use the graded bracket and include $i=j$. Taylor factors are nonzero in characteristic zero. Brackets of an even flavor with itself vanish identically. These are therefore all pairwise graded commutator equations on the five input letters.

The $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ bracket identifies antisymmetric pairs of color slots with one adjoint slot. The quadratic relations thus exchange the two color indices of *any* two factors with unit sign when their ordered flavor labels are held fixed. After all such exchanges the $p$ color slots are symmetric, and the flavor slots are symmetric with the signs of their three odd generators. Conversely the multiplication map to these two symmetric powers has exactly these exchanges as its kernel: tensor representatives can be ordered in their two slot sets independently, and swapping two identical odd flavors gives zero. Hence before singlet projection this quotient is
$$
\mathrm{Sym}^p(\mathfrak{sl}_2^*)\otimes
\mathrm{Sym}^p_{\rm super}(\mathbb C^{2|3}).
$$
In the second factor the even generators represent $x,y$ and the three odd ones represent $\partial_i c$. The singlets of the first factor are generated by powers of the quadratic color norm, as follows by diagonalizing a generic traceless matrix and imposing the Weyl sign. Thus at each maximal-length multidegree the dimension is one if $p$ is even and each holomorphic weight is zero or one. It is zero otherwise.

For every nonzero class pair its even number of first letters in any order and form the product of their quadratic traces. Evaluating on multiples of $H=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ gives the flavor monomial times $2^{p/2}$, nonzero since each odd flavor occurs at most once. All the quadratic pairings are strict by the quadratic theorem, and marked multiplication supplies finite closed completions. At page $r$, the differential raises length by one, increases each holomorphic weight by $r$ and decreases each odd weight by $r$. Thus $|\mu|$ increases by $r$. Incoming relative sources for $r=1$ are on the same maximal-length face and have closed completions. For $r\ge2$ an incoming source would have length strictly exceeding its total coordinate weight. This proves permanence, which also follows from diagonal detection on the regular Cartan locus. The additive absolute splitting gives a permanent absolute copy and its constant-ghost partners. ## The maximal-length sector at arbitrary matrix rank

For $\mathfrak{gl}_N$ the same weight-two boundaries impose pairwise graded commutation of the five first-mode matrices. Write $B=\mathbb C[x,y]\otimes\Lambda(\partial_1c,\partial_2c,\partial_3c)$ for the first-mode algebra of a *single diagonal color*. At all coordinate weights the relative maximal-length answer is
$$
(B^{\otimes N})^{S_N}
$$
with the signed permutation action on odd variables. For $\mathfrak{sl}_N$ set the five sums of the diagonal entries to zero. These equalities hold scheme-theoretically for invariant polynomials, including all exterior classes.

Indeed complete reducibility expresses each singlet in the quotient by brackets as a sum of products of traces. The invariant permutation contractions give these products before passing to the quotient, also for graded entries. In each trace the bracket relations reduce any matrix word to a monomial of $B$ evaluated on the five commuting matrices. For homogeneous monomials $a_1,\ldots,a_k$ of positive degree in $B$, take the alternating permutation color contraction of these $k$ matrix words. A permutation gives one trace for each of its cycles, with its permutation sign and the Koszul sign for moving the odd words into cycle order. Its identity-permutation term is $\operatorname{tr}(a_1)\cdots\operatorname{tr}(a_k)$. The other terms have fewer traces of products of monomials. Induction on $k$ therefore spans all trace products by these alternating contractions. For $k>N$ the alternating endomorphism on $(\mathbb C^N)^{\otimes k}$ is zero.

On diagonal matrices the alternating contraction equals
$$
\sum_{\substack{i_1,\ldots,i_k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\\
\mathrm{distinct\ eigenvalue\ labels}}}
a_1^{(i_1)}\cdots a_k^{(i_k)} .
$$
For fixed indices, the permissible permutations preserve each equal-label block. Bringing their odd factors back to input order cancels the cycle-order Koszul sign. The signed permutation sum on a block therefore vanishes unless it has size one. If two $a_j$ are the same odd monomial, the whole contraction also vanishes globally by exchanging their slots. For the remaining multisets of positive-degree monomials and $k\le N$, the restricted orbit sums are independent: different multisets have disjoint monomial supports, and the coefficient of an ordered allocation is a nonzero factorial for repetitions among the even monomials. These orbit sums also span diagonal invariants by signed averaging. This proves injectivity and surjectivity. The scalar-matrix summand of $\mathfrak{gl}_N$ is a free independent copy of $B$, giving the stated traceless quotient.

Each trace word in this quotient is a nonzero signed multinomial coefficient times a trace-descendant coefficient, or is zero. Indeed in $dC$ at the odd origin the five first modes multiply $d\theta_1,d\theta_2,dz^1,dz^2,dz^3$ respectively. After the graded bracket relations all permutations yielding the specified coefficient agree with the ordered trace up to the common scalar sign; the repeated odd cases vanish on both sides. Marked multiplication therefore gives a finite closed lift of every class. The preceding maximal-length argument excludes incoming differentials. On diagonal jets this identifies the complete relative sector with the Weyl-invariant first modes of separated Cartan colors, including their odd ghost directions.

If $a$ runs over the monomial basis of $B$, including the unit, write $w(a)$ for its five-coordinate weight monomial and let $q$ count colors. Counting ordinary dimensions of both parities gives
$$
\sum_{\substack{N\ge0\\\mathrm{matrix\ ranks}}}q^N
\operatorname{Hilb}\big((B^{\otimes N})^{S_N}\big)
=
\prod_{a\ {\rm even}}(1-qw(a))^{-1}
\prod_{a\ {\rm odd}}(1+qw(a)).
$$
At fixed $N\ge1$ divide the coefficient by $\operatorname{Hilb}(B)$ for traceless matrices. The exact trace replay evaluates the signed permutation contractions on diagonal and off-diagonal Grassmann-valued matrices at ranks two and three, including the first vanishing alternator at each rank. ## Symplectic and odd orthogonal colors

The every-rank lifting argument on this same maximal-length sector also works for $\mathfrak{sp}_{2N}$ and $\mathfrak{so}_{2N+1}$. In the defining representation every adjoint field has two fundamental slots after using the invariant pairing. Symplectic invariant tensors of fundamental slots are spanned by pairings. For odd orthogonal color, special orthogonal conjugation on tuples of adjoints has the same invariants as the full orthogonal group, since the missing component is obtained by multiplying by the central matrix $-1$. Orthogonal invariant tensors are also spanned by pairings. Following the resulting closed contractions expresses each invariant as a product of traces of words, with adjoints for reversed matrix slots; every fundamental matrix field is skew-adjoint, so reversal introduces only the corresponding signs. These pairing statements apply after imposing coefficient Koszul symmetries, since each finite-degree invariant quotient is exact. Pairwise graded commutation in the maximal-length quotient reduces each word to the polarization of a single $\operatorname{Tr}((dC)^k)$ as above. Thus every class has a finite marked lift and the same weight argument excludes incoming differentials. For even special orthogonal color a single volume tensor can produce further Pfaffian contractions, which this trace argument does not classify.

For symplectic color one also obtains the complete dimension series. Let $J$ be the defining nondegenerate skew form. A graded-commuting word $a$ in the five skew-adjoint matrices satisfies $a^{\mathsf T}J=(-1)^{\deg a}Ja$, after moving the graded factors back to input order. In particular every odd-total-length trace is zero. For each homogeneous positive even-total-length word $a_j$ introduce an independent parameter $s_j$ of its coefficient parity, so $s_j a_j$ is an even matrix. Then $T=\sum_j s_j a_j$ is self-adjoint for $J$, and
$$
\exp\!\left(\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\log(1-T)\right)
=\frac{\operatorname{Pf}(J-JT)}{\operatorname{Pf}(J)} .
$$
Indeed both sides square to $\det(1-T)$ and have constant term one. The right side has parameter degree at most $N$. Extracting the ordered multilinear coefficient in $s_1,\ldots,s_k$ gives the signed distinct-label relation for the normalized characters $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(a_j)$ whenever $k>N$: expanding the logarithm gives one cyclic product per cycle, and commutation reduces its trace to the trace of the product word. On a symplectic Cartan the eigenvalues occur in opposite pairs; each such normalized positive word evaluates to its sum over the $N$ pairs. The disjoint-support and orbit-sum argument therefore applies to the subalgebra of $B$ spanned by words of **even total length**. The maximal-length cohomology is precisely the Weyl-invariant algebra of the five Cartan first modes. Its series is the rank-$N$ coefficient of the preceding color product with only even-total-length monomials $a$ included, still distinguishing the coefficient parity from total length. The factor $1/2$ here counts the two defining eigenvalues in a pair.

## Orthogonal Cartan restriction

For $SO_m$ in characteristic zero the restriction theorem for the ordinary scheme of commuting $k$-tuples identifies its invariant coordinate ring with the Weyl-invariant functions on $k$ Cartans, for every $k$. The scheme version is needed here because the brackets generate the actual boundary ideal. [Song, Xia and Xu, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03147)

This extends to the five graded first modes by multilinearization. At homogeneous field length $p$, begin with $p$ distinct ordinary matrix variables and take the part of degree one in each. The free tensor space has an $S_p$ action by relabelling. Its bracket ideal in this component is spanned by one bracket of two distinct slots with arbitrary contractions of the remaining slots. For a given graded flavor content, take the signed projector on each repeated-flavor block (symmetrizer for even flavors, alternator for odd flavors). Its image gives exactly the desired graded component: the same-flavor odd bracket comes from alternating two distinct ordinary bracket slots. Taking this projector commutes with invariant projection, the quotient by the bracket ideal, and Cartan restriction. The ordinary restriction isomorphism is $S_p$-equivariant, so it remains an isomorphism after these projectors. This proves that the classical relative maximal-length quotient for $SO_m$ equals the full Weyl-invariant first-mode algebra, also in type $D$.

For both types the dimensions can consequently be computed by the finite group average. Writing $u,v$ for $x,y$ weights, $t_1,t_2,t_3$ for the three ghost-jet weights and $w$ for the action on the Cartan,
$$
\frac{1}{|W|}
\sum_{\substack{w\in W\\\mathrm{group\ elements}}}
\frac{\det(1+t_1w)\det(1+t_2w)\det(1+t_3w)}
{\det(1-uw)\det(1-vw)} .
$$
In type $B$ all these classes have the finite trace lifts above. In type $D$, each possible one-loop differential *out of* maximal length lands at maximal length and vanishes on the regular Cartan by localization. Cartan restriction is injective on its target, hence $d_1=0$ there, including on the volume-tensor classes. In the same sector every higher incoming differential is excluded by weight. For a source with $\min(\mu_4,\mu_5)\le1$, there is no higher outgoing target of nonnegative odd weights either. This proves permanence in those type-$D$ sectors at every rank.
