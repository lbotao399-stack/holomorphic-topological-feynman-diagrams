# Holomorphic scalar jets on the regular semisimple locus

Write $x_i,y_i$ for the ordered diagonal entries of a commuting traceless pair. Suppose $\Delta=\prod_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)^2$ is invertible. The regular Weyl-invariant functions in all finite holomorphic eigenvalue jets are generated by $\Delta^{-1}$ and the jets of
$$
\operatorname{tr}(x^j),\quad 2\le j\le N,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(x^a y),\quad 1\le a\le N-1 .
$$
Both types are strict quantum cocycles. The second is the coefficient of $s$ in $\operatorname{tr}(x+sy)^{a+1}$ divided by $a+1$. The reciprocal discriminant is invariant and depends only on $x$, so the two-derivative odd factor kills its positive-loop graphs too.

To see the generation, adjoin the distinct roots $x_i$ of the characteristic polynomial. The equations $\sum_i x_i^a y_i=\operatorname{tr}(x^a y)$, including the zero trace at $a=0$, are an invertible Vandermonde system for $y_i$. Use $x_N=-\sum_{i<N}x_i$ and the analogous equation for $y_N$. The full Jacobian in the remaining variables is block triangular with diagonal blocks $(j(x_i^{j-1}-x_N^{j-1}))_{j,i}$ and $(x_i^a-x_N^a)_{a,i}$. Its determinant has magnitude $N!|\Delta|$. At each further jet order the highest derivative has the same coefficient matrix, including mixed derivatives in the three holomorphic directions. Induction reconstructs all ordered jets rationally with denominators powers of the Vandermonde. For a Weyl invariant the denominators can be cleared by a power of $\Delta$; the remaining coefficients symmetric in the roots are polynomials in the characteristic coefficients.

Replace ordinary multiplications by [marked products](README.md). Rational functions of $x$ can have arbitrarily high $x$ derivatives, but each positive loop still consumes a $y$ letter. Each lift therefore terminates by its $y$-degree. On any diagonal arc set $b=c=0$. Every classical variation evaluates to zero; every $Q_n$ graph has $n+2\ge3$ output factors each of odd-coordinate degree one. Their product vanishes in the two odd coordinates, including with differentiated fields and input denominators. Restriction is consequently a chain map to the diagonal arcs with zero differential. The finite jet coordinate construction gives a linear section on Weyl-invariant regular functions. For translation-invariant integrated densities, if restriction is a divergence of regular ordered-arc functions, averaging its primitives over the Weyl group makes them invariant, proving injectivity modulo total derivatives on this locus.

## A mixed jet with a global polynomial representative

For a fixed derivative direction $i$, the polynomial
$$
\langle x,\partial_i y\rangle-\langle y,\partial_i x\rangle
+2\langle b,\partial_i c\rangle
$$
is a strict local quantum cocycle for every invariant nondegenerate action pairing. In $\widehat Q_0$ the inhomogeneous scalar variation gives $-2\langle[x,y],\partial_i c\rangle$, canceled by the $[x,y]$ term of $\widehat Q_0 b$. Only one box can hit a bilinear. Each of the three bilinear terms saturates the odd measure on its input slots, leaving three ghost outputs. Its contracted color trace is $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{ad}}(\operatorname{ad}_{u}\operatorname{ad}_{v}\operatorname{ad}_{w})$, alternating by skew-adjointness and cyclicity. In each term of the linear displacement insertion, two output slots have no additional derivative. Exchanging those slots changes the sign of the color trace; their ghost sign and momentum-determinant sign cancel. Thus each bilinear has zero box separately. The integrated charge has a direct Hamiltonian interpretation. The $\theta_1\theta_2$ component of $\langle C,\partial_i C\rangle$ is
$$
\langle c,\partial_i b\rangle+\langle b,\partial_i c\rangle
+\langle x,\partial_i y\rangle-\langle y,\partial_i x\rangle .
$$
Its difference from the local cocycle is $\partial_i\langle c,b\rangle$, since $c,b$ are odd. Under a variation of the odd field $C$, integration by parts and interchanging the two odd factors give
$$
\delta\frac12\int d^2\theta\,\langle C,\partial_i C\rangle
=\int d^2\theta\,\langle\delta C,\partial_i C\rangle
\qquad\pmod{\text{total derivatives}}.
$$
Thus half the integrated cocycle generates holomorphic translation of $C$ under the constant superfield pairing. The vanishing graph corrections above give a strict local representative for this charge.

In $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ set $x=\operatorname{diag}(w,-w)$, $y=\operatorname{diag}(w^{-1},-w^{-1})$, $w=e^{z^i}$. The mixed cocycle restricts to $-4$, so it survives integration. A total derivative on these regular Laurent data has zero constant coefficient. More generally on the diagonal $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ locus its restriction is
$$
\partial_i\operatorname{tr}(xy)
-\frac{\operatorname{tr}(xy)}{\operatorname{tr}(x^2)}\,\partial_i\operatorname{tr}(x^2).
$$
Globally the derivative of the undifferentiated bidegree-$(1,1)$ invariant gives the symmetric combination of the two scalar derivatives, whereas the displayed polynomial cocycle supplies the alternating combination on commuting arcs.
