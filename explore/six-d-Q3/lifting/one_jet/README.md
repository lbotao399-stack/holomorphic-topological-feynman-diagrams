# One holomorphic derivative for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$

Fix one holomorphic direction and let a prime denote its derivative. Every classical local cohomology class of derivative degree one has a finite quantum lift. All survive every incoming differential. The statement includes arbitrary field lengths, both odd source weights and all powers of the antifield permitted by those weights.

## Classical filtration

Relative to constant colors put $x=g_1$, $y=g_2$ and use the supercharge $\widehat Q_0=Q_0/i$. There is exactly one differentiated field. From $\widehat Q_0b=[x,y]$ and differentiation of the full covariant equations,
$$
\widehat Q_0 c'=0,\qquad
\widehat Q_0 x'=[c',x],\qquad
\widehat Q_0 y'=[c',y],\qquad
\widehat Q_0 b'=[c',b]+[x',y]+[x,y'] .
$$
Filter by the total odd-coordinate weight carried by this differentiated field: $c'$ has weight zero, $x',y'$ have weight one and $b'$ has weight two. The associated graded differential acts only on the undifferentiated spectators, through the Koszul complex $K$ of the three components of $[x,y]$. Its invariant cohomology with a jet spectator of a fixed species is $(H(K)\otimes\mathfrak{sl}_2)^{\mathfrak{sl}_2}$, using the trace pairing.

Let $S$ be the polynomial ring in the six components of $x,y$. The homology of $K$ has $H_0=S/([x,y])$. Its $H_1$ is generated by $U=\operatorname{tr}(xb)$ and $V=\operatorname{tr}(yb)$, with the three component relations $y^j U=x^j V$, in a fixed color basis with component index $j$. Both higher groups vanish. Each minor annihilates $H(K)$ since multiplication by a Koszul generator contracts its differential. The polynomial color covariants of $x,y$ are generated by $x,y,[x,y]$ over the invariant scalar ring. Invariant projection is exact at each bidegree. Hence the symbols on the first page are spanned by pairings of $x$ or $y$ with a differentiated letter, multiplied by an undifferentiated invariant and, in $H_1$, by $U$ or $V$. [Koszul calculation and covariants](../sl2_zero_jets.md)

Each pairing has a closed representative of the required leading filtration degree. With $\langle\ ,\ \rangle=\operatorname{tr}(\,\cdot\,\cdot\,)$, these are
$$
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\text{differentiated field}&x&y\\ \hline
c'&\langle x,c'\rangle&\langle y,c'\rangle\\
x'&\langle x,x'\rangle&\langle y,x'\rangle-\langle b,c'\rangle\\
y'&\langle x,y'\rangle+\langle b,c'\rangle&\langle y,y'\rangle\\
b'&\partial U&\partial V
\end{array}
$$
Indeed $\widehat Q_0\langle x,y'\rangle=-\langle[x,y],c'\rangle$, $\widehat Q_0\langle y,x'\rangle=\langle[x,y],c'\rangle$ and $\widehat Q_0\langle b,c'\rangle=\langle[x,y],c'\rangle$. In $\partial U=\langle x,b'\rangle+\langle x',b\rangle$ and in $\partial V$ the correction has lower filtration. Products with the undifferentiated generators give closed representatives of every associated graded class. Thus this three-step classical filtration collapses at its first cohomology page.

## Multiplicities

At bidegree $(a,b)$ the coefficient space of $H_0$ is the symmetric power $\mathrm{Sym}^{a+b}(\mathfrak{sl}_2^*)$. Indeed the three minors allow an $x$ and a $y$ to exchange their color indices, so monomials are determined by the combined multiset of colors. The substitution $x=t h,y=s h$ with two scalar variables $t,s$ and an arbitrary color vector $h$ separates all these monomials.

For $H_1$, remove the two weights $(1,1)$ of its single $b$ and let $m=a+b-2$. A nonempty component requires $a,b\ge1$ and $m\ge1$. The coefficients of $U$ and $V$ each carry a symmetric multiset of $m-1$ color slots. When both types of coefficient occur, the relation $y^jU=x^jV$ identifies their multisets. Substitution on $x=t h,y=s h$ takes the resulting class to $t^{a-1}s^{b-1}\operatorname{tr}(h b_{\rm field})$ times that same polynomial in $h$, with $b_{\rm field}$ the original odd field. This is injective and gives one copy of $\mathrm{Sym}^{m-1}(\mathfrak{sl}_2^*)$.

One differentiated adjoint letter selects spin one among these symmetric powers. A homogeneous equivariant map from the single color vector $h$ to $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ is a multiple of $h$ times a polynomial in $\operatorname{tr}(h^2)$ by the preceding covariant calculation. Thus spin one occurs precisely once at odd symmetric degree. Let $l$ record field length and $u,v$ record odd-coordinate weights. The relative dimension series in the chosen derivative direction is
$$
l(1+u)(1+v)
\left[
\frac{l(u+v)}{(1-l^2u^2)(1-l^2v^2)}
+l uv\left(\frac{1+l^2uv}{(1-l^2u^2)(1-l^2v^2)}-1\right)
\right].
$$
The outer factor chooses $c',x',y',b'$. The first summand counts the odd powers in $H_0$, and the second counts $a,b\ge1$ with $m$ positive and even in $H_1$. For absolute cohomology multiply this series by $1+l^3$. At each finite multidegree the relative complex splits equivariantly into its cohomology and contractible pairs before taking constant-color Lie algebra cohomology. The extra copy is given by $\operatorname{tr}(c^3)$.

## Quantum lifts

The displayed pairing representatives are strict quadratic cocycles. In the mixed entries the scalar derivative and $\langle b,c'\rangle$ have zero box separately; $\partial U,\partial V$ follow by translation invariance. The undifferentiated generators and the absolute factor $\operatorname{tr}(c^3)$ are strict. Marked multiplication therefore gives finite lifts of every classical product used above. All positive-page incoming differentials raise the holomorphic derivative degree by $3r$ at page $r$. The specified source degree one admits none. The exact replay builds the full relative differential on monomials with one differentiated field in the two adjacent Cartan weights. Subtracting the second cohomology multiplicity from the first selects spin zero. At all 36 odd-coordinate bidegrees $0\le p,q\le5$ this gives the 97 relative classes predicted by the displayed series, resolved separately by field length. Each matrix square vanishes.
