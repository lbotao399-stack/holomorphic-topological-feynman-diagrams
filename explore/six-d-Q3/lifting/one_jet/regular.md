# All jet orders on the regular semisimple scalar locus

For $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_N$ put $x=g_1$, $y=g_2$. Invert the discriminant $\Delta$ of the matrix $x(0)$. In constant-color relative cohomology the independent generators on the ordered-eigenvalue cover are the traceless diagonal jets of $x,y,b$ in every order, and the diagonal ghost jets of positive order. The $b$ and ghost generators are exterior. Take simultaneous permutations of the eigenvalue labels to return to the original locus. Absolute cohomology has the additional tensor factor $H^*(\mathfrak{sl}_N,\mathbb C)$. Each classical class has a finite meromorphic quantum lift. Every differential of the quantum spectral sequence on this locus vanishes.

## Finite jet slice

For the classical calculation fix jet order $M$ and set $A=\mathbb C[z^1,z^2,z^3]/(z^1,z^2,z^3)^{M+1}$, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. Collect jets in matrix-valued Taylor polynomials in $A$. In the relative complex $c\in\mathfrak g\otimes\mathfrak m$, while $x,y,b\in\mathfrak g\otimes A$. Write $D=\widehat Q_0$ for the reduced differential. The equations are
$$
Dc=c^2,\qquad Dx=[c,x],\qquad Dy=[c,y],\qquad Db=[c,b]+[x,y].
$$
Constant colors act simultaneously on all four matrices. On the ordered cover put $x(0)=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N)$. The residual constant group is the diagonal torus. This restriction computes the constant-color invariants: the ordered eigenline projectors are $\prod_{j\ne i}(x(0)-\lambda_j)/(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)$, so frames of ordered eigenlines differ exactly by the torus. Its invariants descend along the frame bundle, and the permutation group descends the eigenvalue cover.

Choose $g=\exp k$ with $k$ off-diagonal and $k(0)=0$ so that $g^{-1}xg$ is diagonal. The exponential is a polynomial through order $M$. Having fixed $k$ below degree $s$, the degree-$s$ off-diagonal coefficient in $g^{-1}xg$ changes by $(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)k^i{}_{j,\alpha}$ at every jet multi-index $\alpha$ of degree $s$. Each difference is a unit. Recursion gives unique coordinates $k$ and the diagonal jets, with the polynomial inverse given by conjugation. This explicitly removes the positive jet group modulo its diagonal stabilizer.

Set $h=g^{-1}xg$ and $\chi=g^{-1}cg-g^{-1}Dg$. Then $Dh=[\chi,h]$ is diagonal. The root differences $h^i{}_i-h^j{}_j$ are units in $A$, so $\chi$ is diagonal and $Dh=0$. Moreover $D\chi=\chi^2=0$. The original positive ghosts can be replaced by $Dk$ and $\chi$: given these, the polynomial derivative of $g=\exp k$ gives $Dg$, and $c=g\chi g^{-1}+(Dg)g^{-1}$. Thus $(k,Dk)$ are independent contractible coordinates, and $\chi(0)=0$.

Write $\widetilde y=g^{-1}yg$, $\widetilde b=g^{-1}bg$. Their differentials are $D\widetilde y=[\chi,\widetilde y]$ and $D\widetilde b=[\chi,\widetilde b]+[h,\widetilde y]$. On root $(i,j)$, $i\ne j$, replace the even coordinates $\widetilde y^i{}_j$ by
$$
D\widetilde b^i{}_j=[\chi,\widetilde b]^i{}_j+(h^i{}_i-h^j{}_j)\widetilde y^i{}_j .
$$
Again the root difference is a unit in $A$. This is an invertible triangular change at every jet order. The pairs $(\widetilde b^i{}_j,D\widetilde b^i{}_j)$ are contractible, while all remaining diagonal fields have zero differential. For each contractible pair $(w,Dw)$ the homotopy $w\partial/\partial(Dw)$ has graded commutator with $D$ equal to its polynomial degree operator. Division by positive degree contracts its nonconstant part for either parity. The diagonal torus acts trivially on the remaining coordinates. This proves the relative assertion, compatibly with increasing $M$.

For the absolute complex take the constant-color Lie algebra cochains with coefficients in the positive-mode complex before taking constant-color invariants. These coefficient modules are locally finite rational representations of $SL_N$: denominators are powers of the invariant $\Delta$, so each numerator still has finite-dimensional orbit span. Complete reducibility splits the complex into cohomology and contractible pairs equivariantly. For a coefficient representation $\rho$ choose an orthonormal basis $(T_j)$ of a compact real form for an invariant positive inner product. On Lie algebra cochains, the odd operator $\sum_{j\ {\rm compact\ basis}}\iota_{T_j}\rho(T_j)$ has anticommutator with the Lie algebra differential equal to $\sum_{j\ {\rm compact\ basis}}\rho(T_j)^2$: the form action in Cartan's insertion identity cancels the commutator on coefficients by invariance of the inner product. In a unitary realization the negative of this Casimir is positive, with kernel exactly the invariant vectors. Dividing by its eigenvalue contracts each nontrivial simple summand. Thus only the trivial summands contribute, tensored with $H^*(\mathfrak{sl}_N,\mathbb C)$. In particular a linear diagonal constant ghost is absent from relative cohomology.

## Global representatives on this open set

For $2\le j\le N$ use $\operatorname{tr}(x^j)$. For $1\le j\le N-1$ use
$$
\operatorname{tr}(x^j y),\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(x^j b),\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(x^j\partial_i c),\quad i=1,2,3 .
$$
These are strict trace descendants. In particular $\operatorname{tr}(x^j b)$ is the $\theta_2$ coefficient in $\operatorname{tr}((\partial_{\theta_1}C)^{j+1})$ divided by $j+1$. The last trace is the coefficient of $(d\theta_1)^j dz^i$ in $\operatorname{tr}((dC)^{j+1})$ at $\theta=0$, divided by $j+1$. Whole-expression holomorphic derivatives are also strict, as are $\Delta^{-1}$, which depends only on $x$, and the undifferentiated constant-ghost representatives of $H^*(\mathfrak{sl}_N,\mathbb C)$. [Odd root cancellations](../single_trace.md)

On the diagonal slice a holomorphic derivative of any of these traces gives the highest-order diagonal jets against powers of $x(0)$ and terms involving previously determined lower jets. For the positive ghost choose any nonzero direction in its derivative multi-index and use the last trace. The trace-zero equation supplies the row of degree zero; the rows $1,\lambda_i,\ldots,\lambda_i^{N-1}$ give a Vandermonde determinant. For $x$ itself the differentiated traces start at $j=2$ and supply the same rows multiplied by their nonzero integer factors. Solve first for every $x$ jet, then for $y,b,c$ jets. This recovers the ordered diagonal variables over the ring with the ordered eigenvalues adjoined. Exterior coefficients obey the same linear equations. The only denominators are powers of eigenvalue differences, hence divide powers of $\Delta$. For an invariant polynomial in these variables, permuting and averaging its expanded expression makes the remaining eigenvalue coefficients symmetric; they are polynomials in the characteristic coefficients of $x(0)$ and $\Delta^{-1}$. Thus ordinary products of the listed strict generators and their jets span classical cohomology on the open set.

## Meromorphic differential and incoming classes

Extend the graph operators to finite-jet polynomials with denominators powers of $\Delta$ by their actual finite-order root functional derivatives. Ordinary differentiation of a denominator is part of this prescription. The quantum differential is higher order and its action on the localization uses these graph derivatives. The Ward identities continue there: at any fixed loop and input jet order they are identities of finite-order differential operators with polynomial coefficients, and such an identity extends to regular functions on the open set by Taylor expansion at each point of the set. The same argument applies to the marked chain identity.

Assign the second odd-coordinate weight zero to $x,c,\Delta^{-1}$ and one to $y,b$. Each $n$-loop differential graph has $2n+1$ cubic vertices and $3n+1$ physical propagators. Each cubic Berezin integral supplies one unit of this weight, while every propagator contracts complementary components of total weight one. Hence $Q_n$ lowers it by $n$. Each order-$m$ binary marked graph lowers it by $m$, from $2m$ vertices and $3m$ physical propagators. Differentiating $\Delta^{-1}$ arbitrarily often costs zero second weight but cannot supply negative second weight. The loop action and any iterated marked lift terminate by this weight, including for meromorphic coefficients. The quantum corrections can use higher holomorphic jets; the finite cutoff above was imposed only in the classical calculation.

Replace each classical product by a fixed bracketing of marked products. The chain identity gives a closed lift with the same leading cochain. This holds for all field lengths on the localized domain, excluding incoming boundaries even from meromorphic primitives. Explicitly, suppose a formal primitive has quantum differential starting at $\hbar^r$ with a classically nonexact coefficient. If $r>0$, its order-zero coefficient is classically closed. Subtract a closed lift of its class and a quantum boundary lifting the difference of the leading representatives. The new primitive starts at order one and has the same differential. Iterating $r$ times forces the putative coefficient to be classically exact. Therefore all nonzero quantum differentials of the global complex restrict to zero on this regular locus.

Apply the construction to any fixed linear polarization of $g_1,g_2$ by a determinant-one change of odd coordinates. On the reduced commuting-pair locus the union of these opens comprises all pairs with distinct joint eigenvalues. Indeed a generic linear combination separates every pair of distinct joint eigenvalues: the excluded polarizations are a finite union of lines. Thus a global quantum differential can survive restriction only where at least two joint eigenvalues coincide. This support statement allows a global differential concentrated on the repeated-eigenvalue locus. [Scalar regular coordinates](../scalar_products/regular_jets.md)

## Complex reductive gauge algebras

Let $\mathfrak g$ be complex reductive with the theory's nondegenerate invariant pairing and connected gauge group $G$. Choose a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and let $W$ be its Weyl group. Invert the invariant discriminant restricting to $\prod_{\alpha>0}\alpha(x(0))^2$ over the positive Cartan roots. The complete relative cohomology on this open set consists of the $W$-invariant polynomials in $\mathfrak h$-valued $x,y,b$ jets and positive-order $c$ jets, with the discriminant inverted. Absolute cohomology tensors this algebra with $H^*(\mathfrak g,\mathbb C)$. All classes lift at every order.

Indeed the regular semisimple orbit slice is the bundle induced from $\mathfrak h_{\rm reg}$ by the normalizer of its Cartan torus; a regular semisimple element determines its unique Cartan centralizer. The preceding recursion works with $k$ in the sum of root spaces tensored with $\mathfrak m$. On the root labeled $\alpha$, conjugation at the next jet order has linear coefficient $\alpha(x(0))$. The group exponential here is the finite nilpotent positive-jet exponential. With $h=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}x$, the transformed ghost $\chi$ is Cartan-valued. On the entire truncated root module the linear coefficient in $D\widetilde b$ is multiplication by $\alpha(h)\in A^\times$. This gives exactly the same contractible pairs, leaving $W$ to act on all Cartan jets. For the absolute complex the center acts trivially on adjoint fields. Its constant ghosts contribute the exterior algebra of the dual center inside $H^*(\mathfrak g,\mathbb C)$, while the semisimple factors use the Casimir contraction above.

Use homogeneous generators $P_j$ of the invariant polynomial algebra, including linear generators on the center. Chevalley restriction identifies their Cartan restrictions with polynomial coordinates on $\mathfrak h/W$. The map from the regular Cartan to its quotient is finite étale: the stabilizer of a regular element in $W$ is trivial, and a free finite group quotient of a smooth complex affine variety is étale. Hence $dP_j(x(0))$ form a basis of the dual Cartan on this cover. The following globally defined strict observables replace the matrix traces:
$$
P_j(x),\qquad dP_j(x)(y),\qquad dP_j(x)(b),\qquad dP_j(x)(\partial_i c).
$$
For each homogeneous invariant $P_j$, its symmetric polarization contracts the factors of $P_j(dC)$. The proof that two ordered root contractions vanish holds before choosing the color tensor. Invariance gives the classical closure. Taking the coefficients in the two odd source differentials, in the odd coordinates and in the holomorphic differentials gives the four displayed observables: for instance $dP_j(x)(b)$ is the $\theta_2$ coefficient of $P_j(\partial_{\theta_1}C)$. Their jets give Cartan-linear systems with leading rows $dP_j(x(0))$. Solving by jet order therefore recovers every ordered Cartan variable. The coefficient ring of the regular Cartan is generated over its invariant ring by the Cartan coordinates; averaging coefficients over $W$ descends each expression in the recovered invariant observables. The invariant coefficient ring has only discriminant denominators. Its inverse has second odd weight zero, so the same graph bound proves finite meromorphic lifts and excludes meromorphic incoming boundaries.

On a Cartan pair the singular set left by all scalar polarizations is precisely
$$
\bigcup_{\alpha\ {\rm roots}}\{(x,y)\in\mathfrak h^2:\ \alpha(x)=\alpha(y)=0\}\big/W .
$$
If no root vanishes on both entries, each root rules out only one line of polarizations, and a polarization outside the finite union is regular. This describes the support bound on the reduced Cartan quotient. For a purely abelian algebra the root set is empty, the discriminant is one, and all loop operators vanish since every cubic color bracket vanishes.
