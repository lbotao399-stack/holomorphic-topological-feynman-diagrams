# Poisson BF triangle on the holomorphic plane of complex dimension two

Use the Li–Liu–Chang triangle with external holomorphic momenta $\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=0$ and projective times $(t_{12},t_{13},t_{23})=(1-r_1-r_2,r_1,r_2)$. Write $[pq]$ for the Poisson determinant of two holomorphic covectors. For the directed loop $1\to2\to3\to1$, use the external field as the first Hamiltonian in each of the three brackets. The internal incoming momenta are $-y_{13},y_{12},y_{23}$. Kirchhoff's currents give

$$
[\lambda_1,-y_{13}]=-t_{23}[\lambda_1\lambda_2],\quad
[\lambda_2,y_{12}]=-t_{13}[\lambda_1\lambda_2],\quad
[\lambda_3,y_{23}]=-t_{12}[\lambda_1\lambda_2].
$$

The source triangle measure is $[\lambda_1\lambda_2]dr_1dr_2$. At zero displacement the resulting ordered geometric coefficient, before the common heat-kernel and BV factors, is

$$
-[\lambda_1\lambda_2]^4\int_{r_1,r_2>0,\ r_1+r_2<1}r_1r_2(1-r_1-r_2)\,dr_1dr_2
=-\frac{[\lambda_1\lambda_2]^4}{120}.
$$

Reversing which internal leg enters the Poisson bracket reverses all three signs. In either orientation the fourth power is invariant under the six external permutations, so antisymmetrization of the three Hamiltonian inputs gives zero. This proves the vanishing of this local bulk anomaly cochain by direct integration. The same three-vertex symbol occurs in a closed loop of the scalar $\beta,\gamma$ matter representation in the action of T1; each flavor multiplicity multiplies the zero alternating cochain.

For separated displacement insertions, retain the source exponential $\exp(-\sum_{e\in\mathrm{edges}} y_e\cdot z_e)$. After extracting the common exponential of the two tree edges, the coefficient of the $i,j$ powers of the two linear terms in its exponent is the same fourth power with minus sign times $(i+1)(j+1)/(i+j+5)!$. This includes every derivative insertion generated by those displacements. ## Local operator with cubic Poisson vertices

At loop number $n\ge1$ a tight graph has $n+1$ interaction vertices and $2n+1$ edges. If the insertion has degree $k$, exactly $k-n+1$ interaction fields survive. With fewer than two surviving fields, all Kirchhoff currents lie on one momentum line and the two-component current Jacobian vanishes, including in the presence of derivative insertions. The two-vertex sparsity bound gives $k\le n+1$. Therefore $k=n+1$ and every interaction vertex has a spoke. Applying sparsity to the cone on any subset of the interaction vertices excludes a cycle in the remaining graph. It has $n$ edges, $n+1$ vertices and maximum degree two, so it is a path. Exactly its endpoints carry surviving fields.

Use the fan orientation and ratios $r_1,\ldots,r_{2n}$ of the [paper](../../sources/LLC_main.tex). The two endpoint momenta are $\lambda_1,\lambda_{n+1}$. For the ordered Poisson factors take $[\lambda_1,-y_{12}]$ on the left, $[y_{i-1,i},-y_{i,i+1}]$ at each interior vertex, and $[y_{n,n+1},\lambda_{n+1}]$ on the right. The three momenta at a vertex sum to zero, so cyclically changing the differentiated pair preserves the factor, and transposing it reverses the sign. This applies to both $B\{A,A\}/2$ and $\beta\{A,\gamma\}$ with the actual differentiated arguments in their action order.

The coefficient of $\lambda_{n+1}$ in $y_{i,i+1}$ is $-r_{2n}\prod_{j=i}^{n-1}(1-r_{2j})$. For $2\le i\le n$ the difference $y_{i-1,i}-(1-r_{2i-2})y_{i,i+1}$ equals $r_{2i-2}(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}r_{2j-1})\lambda_1$. Inserting these in the brackets above and multiplying by the paper's fan measure gives the ordered kernel density
$$
[\lambda_1\lambda_{n+1}]^{2n+1}
r_{2n-1}r_{2n}^{2n-1}
\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}
r_{2j-1}^{2(n-j)+1}r_{2j}^{2}(1-r_{2j})^{2j-1}\,d^{2n}r.
$$
The last two coordinates occupy the unit two-simplex. Their integral is $(2n-1)!/(2n+2)!$. Each preceding pair contributes $1/[(n-j+1)(2j)(2j+1)(2j+2)]$. At zero displacement the labeled geometric kernel is therefore
$$
\frac{[\lambda_1\lambda_{n+1}]^{2n+1}}
{2^n n!(n+1)!(2n+1)!}.
$$
At $n=1,2,3$ the denominators are $24$, $5760$, $5806080$. The BV pairing, vertex and Wick signs, flavor contractions, and automorphism weights of a specified operator multiply this ordered kernel. It acts on $n+1$ input fields and produces two output fields. In particular a monomial of length $k$ receives no correction from $n\ge k$.

**Arbitrary input jets.** Keep the edge exponential of the paper. After dividing the density by its integral, the successive odd coordinates have beta distributions with parameters $(2(n-j)+2,1)$, the even coordinates have parameters $(3,2j)$, and the final two coordinates and their complement have Dirichlet parameters $(2,2n,1)$. For directional displacement Taylor coefficients, conservation first replaces each path displacement by $z_{i,i+1}+z_{i+1,n+2}-z_{i,n+2}$ and produces the two endpoint spoke phases. Eliminate the path from the left. The scalar multiplying $\lambda_1$ in the transferred source gets a factor $r_{2i-1}$, and the displacement transferred to the next path current gets a factor $1-r_{2i}$. The local exponential supplies the remaining factor $r_{2i-1}r_{2i}$. Integrating these powers with the beta moments leaves a polynomial in the accumulated source multiplier and the two endpoint contractions of the carried displacement. The number of polynomial variables stays three as $n$ increases. Polarization recovers mixed jets. The replay checks the signed density against grounded Kirchhoff currents and the full Jacobian through seven loops, and checks 14 displaced coefficients by direct polynomial integration. ## Ternary stress tensor bracket

Use the connected coefficient of the identity and the improved bilinear stress tensor of Bomans–Wu §4 for a Lagrangian superconformal theory with its superconformal R-charge fixed. Count the $c,\gamma$ fields minus the $b,\beta$ fields. Each propagator removes one of each. Each stress insertion has difference zero, each $b[c,c]$ or $\beta[c,\gamma]$ vertex has difference one, and each nonconstant monomial of degree $m$ in $W(\gamma)$ has difference $m>0$. A complete Wick contraction therefore contains zero bulk interaction vertices. Three normal-ordered bilinears have just the two directed connected triangles. Every graph of higher loop number has zero coefficient in this bracket.

For one chiral field let $\zeta$ be half its scalar R-charge as in §4. In the directed triangle, the incoming currents are $-y_{13},y_{12},y_{23}$. At the three insertions add $\zeta\lambda_i$ to the incoming current, since the derivative in the improvement acts on the entire insertion. The opposite direction replaces each improved incoming current by its negative at $1-\zeta$. Take the difference of the two cubic polynomials with the Wick sign and integrate against $[\lambda_1\lambda_2]dr_1dr_2$. In the paper's normalization, fixed by the free vector bracket $\Lambda_{\rm vector}$, the contribution per chiral is
$$
\frac32\zeta(1-\zeta)(1-2\zeta)\Lambda_{\mathcal N=4}-(1-2\zeta)\Lambda_{\rm vector}.
$$
For a vector multiplet add $\dim\mathfrak g$ copies of the vector bracket. Each chiral contributes its representation dimension times the displayed expression. All eight tensor components follow from simplex moments. Each term in the two tensors contains the momentum of at least one insertion in its own free slot. Contracting all three supercurrents with Hamiltonian vector fields therefore kills both tensors. This identifies the restriction seen by the Poisson triangle.

For $\mathcal N=4$ Yang–Mills the three adjoint chirals have $\zeta=1/3$. The two coefficients, per adjoint component, are $3(1/9)=1/3$ and $1+3(-1/3)=0$, respectively. Thus the connected central bracket is $(\dim\mathfrak g)\Lambda_{\mathcal N=4}/3$, with $a=c=\dim\mathfrak g/4$. A single free vector gives $16(a-c)=1$, fixing the sign in favor of (3.14) over (1.14). The middle epsilon in (3.15) has its free index fixed by the same three-slot tensor printed in §2; the replay uses the displayed cyclic index structure.
