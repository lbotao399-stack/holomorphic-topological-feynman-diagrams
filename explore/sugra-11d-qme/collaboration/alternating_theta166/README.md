# Fully polarized $\theta_{166}$ on a transverse input

On the following external waves, the sum over all placements and field orientations of $\theta_{166}$ is $-12644\pi/892812375$ in the ordered kernel normalization, with $g$ removed. Dividing by the graph automorphism order gives $-3161\pi/892812375$.

## External waves

In the coordinate basis $e_i$ and dual basis $e^i$, the even one-form $\gamma$ has momentum $e^1$ and polarization $e^2$. The nine odd vectors $\mu$ have momenta and polarizations in this exterior order:

$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrr}
p & e^2&e^3&e^4&e^5&-e^1&-e^2&-e^4&-e^3&-e^5\\
u & e_3&e_4&e_5&e_2&e_2&e_1&e_1&e_1&e_1
\end{array}
$$

Each $p_a u^a=0$, the sum of the ten momenta vanishes, and $\nu=0$. The trivalent endpoints carry zero external momentum. The source order of the thirteen edges is the short path $A$, then the six edges of $B$, then the six of $C$, all drawn from $N$ to $N'$. The coefficient of the product of nine independent odd parameters in the external order above is the alternating sum with no division by $9!$. The Wick sign from the thirteen edge contractions is already included in each oriented diagram. ## Exterior sum

Write $A_{B_j}$ for the integrated alternating coefficient with $\gamma$ at the $j$th interior vertex of $B$. The geometric determinant is $D_{166}=[B_1\cdots B_5][C_1\cdots C_5]$, with each bracket the determinant of the five path momenta. Each path must receive one wave on each coordinate line. At a fixed $\gamma$ slot the surviving assignments and the sign from its exterior order are

$$
2^4\,4!\,5!=46080,\qquad
\operatorname{sgn}(\text{nine odd labels})D_{166}=(-1)^{j-1+k},
$$

where $k$ counts the negative waves on coordinate lines $2,3,4,5$ placed on $B$. For $j=1$ and all positive waves on $B$, ordering each path by coordinate interchanges the third and fourth negative waves in the exterior order. This contributes $-1$, and $D_{166}=-1$. Each subsequent exchange of a positive and negative wave on one coordinate changes the exterior sign. Moving $\gamma$ to $B_j$ changes the path determinant by $(-1)^{j-1}$. Reordering either path's remaining waves changes the determinant and the exterior sign equally.

The unique short edge identifies the endpoints. The four graph automorphisms are generated by exchanging the two long paths and simultaneously reversing all three paths. Exchange interchanges two six-edge blocks, and reversal reverses two six-edge blocks. Thus both preserve the edge permutation sign. On the ten interior slots, exchange has sign $-1$ and moves $\gamma$ by five positions, so its permutation of the nine odd slots is even. Reversal has sign $+1$ on the ten slots and moves $\gamma$ by an even number of positions. Both preserve $D_{166}$ and transport the edge parameters with their edges. The integrated $\gamma$-placement orbits consequently have sizes $4,4,2$.

## Integration and replay

The signed Wick polynomials have degree at most twelve in the first currents of $B,C$. Grouping the assignments by the relative ordering of the axes on the two long paths gives $5!=120$ polynomials per representative. Each has $2^4\,4!=384$ assignments and four admissible field orientations per assignment. Integration uses the two independent uniform path simplices and the conductance fractions $(w_A,w_B,w_C)$ with Dirichlet parameters $(11/2,1/2,1/2)$. In particular, for the decreasing tail fractions $t_1,\ldots,t_5$ on a six-edge path and nonnegative powers $k_1,\ldots,k_5$, the normalized moment is

$$
\frac{5!}{(1+k_5)(2+k_4+k_5)(3+k_3+k_4+k_5)(4+k_2+k_3+k_4+k_5)(5+k_1+k_2+k_3+k_4+k_5)}.
$$

One implementation expands the conductance factors $w_B-1$ and $1-w_C$ directly. The other uses $1-w_B=w_A+w_C$ and $1-w_C=w_A+w_B$ and sums positive Dirichlet moments with the external signs. Both give the same exact rational for each of the 360 polynomials. Two evaluations over $\mathbb F_{1009}$ also compare each assembled polynomial with the direct contractions of the two original cubic words in the physical coordinate basis. Dropping the exterior sign changes all 720 comparisons.
