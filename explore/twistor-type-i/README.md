# Two-loop WZW₄ on flat space

Costello §4.3 leaves open the first nonredundant action logarithm in four-dimensional WZW₄. Proposition 11.0.1 computes a two-loop composite-operator OPE. For SO(8) the periodic-RG theorem includes the closed gravitational field. For a group with an irreducible adjoint quartic, (2.3.1) supplies a term local on spacetime and bilocal on twistor space. The calculations below use pure WZW₄. [Costello, (1.1.2), §§2.2–2.3, 4.3, 11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08879) · [WZW₄ action and field equation, (2.26)–(2.31)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00940)

## Two-quartic sunset

Use the coefficient of $\int\operatorname{tr}(J\wedge *J)$ in (1.1.2), namely one. In $\sigma=e^\phi$ coordinates the kinetic quartic is

$$
-\frac1{12}\int\operatorname{tr}[\phi,\partial_\mu\phi][\phi,\partial^\mu\phi].
$$

The Wess–Zumino term is odd under inversion. Write $g_{ab}$ for the trace pairing and lower the Lie bracket with it to give $f_{abc}$. The quadratic Casimir in this pairing obeys $f_{amn}f_b{}^{mn}=C_Ag_{ab}$. For the external field in slot 0, let $p,k_1,k_2,k_3$ be the incoming momenta. Define the three channel factors by cyclic permutation of internal slots in

$$
C_1=f_{01m}f_{23}{}^m,\qquad X_1=(k_1-p)\cdot(k_3-k_2).
$$

Here the numerals on $f$ stand for the colors of the indicated legs. Four functional derivatives give $\tfrac16(C_1X_1+C_2X_2+C_3X_3)$ in Fourier space. Both triples sum to zero. Contracting the internal colors gives $C_A^2g_{ab}$ on the diagonal and $-C_A^2g_{ab}/2$ off the diagonal. Permuting the three integration momenta thus gives $C_A^2g_{ab}X_1^2/8$ under the common integral.

In $d=4-2\epsilon$ let $I_0$ denote the integral with three massless propagator denominators and two measures $d^dk/(2\pi)^d$. For $\nu=d/2-1$,

$$
I_0=\frac{(p^2)^{d-3}}{(4\pi)^d}
\frac{\Gamma(\nu)^3\Gamma(3-d)}{\Gamma(3\nu)},\qquad
[\epsilon^{-1}]I_0=-\frac{p^2}{4(4\pi)^4}.
$$

Brackets $[\epsilon^{-1}]$ extract the simple-pole coefficient. With $\langle\ \rangle$ denoting the same integral divided by $I_0$, cancellation of a propagator leaves a scaleless integral. Hence

$$
X_1=2p\cdot(k_2-k_3)+k_2^2-k_3^2,\qquad
\frac{\langle X_1^2\rangle}{(p^2)^2}
=\frac{2}{3(3\nu+1)}.
$$

For a direct reduction, at fixed $l=k_2+k_3$ the normalized bubble tensor with numerator $(k_2-k_3)^\mu(k_2-k_3)^\nu$ is $(l^\mu l^\nu-\delta^{\mu\nu}l^2)/(d-1)$. Its trace vanishes upon canceling scaleless terms except for $-l^2$; contraction with $l_\mu$ vanishes. For $B(q)=\int d^dl\,(l^2)^q/(l+p)^2$, the remaining double-propagator integral gives

$$
\frac{B(q+1)}{p^2 B(q)}=\frac{q+1}{q+d-1},\qquad q=\nu-1 .
$$

The inverse two-point function carries the connected cumulant factor $-1/3!$ and three propagators each with normalization $1/2$. With these factors the Euclidean graph gives

$$
[\epsilon^{-1}]\Gamma^{(2)}_{ab}(p)\big|_{4,4}
=\left(-\frac1{3!}\right)\frac18\frac{C_A^2g_{ab}}8
\frac{(p^2)^2}{6}\frac{-p^2}{4(4\pi)^4}
=\frac{C_A^2g_{ab}(p^2)^3}{9216(4\pi)^4}.
$$

Multiplying the kinetic action by a constant multiplies this graph by its inverse. The residue is proportional to the linear equation of motion and is removable by a local field redefinition. [Scalar, momentum-space tensor, and position-space tensor replay](sunset.py)

## Three external fields on shell

In four dimensions a connected two-loop graph of two-derivative vertices has superficial degree $4L+2V-2I=6$, with $I=V+L-1$ internal lines. On conserved complex null momenta $p_1,p_2,p_3$, all $p_i\cdot p_j$ vanish. A $U(2)$-invariant polynomial is generated by pairings of holomorphic with antiholomorphic momenta. Their symmetric parts vanish, and their antisymmetric parts have a single cyclic value, the Kähler pairing $\omega(p_1,p_2)=\omega(p_2,p_3)=\omega(p_3,p_1)$. Thus the local six-derivative three-point polynomial is a scalar multiple of $\omega(p_1,p_2)^3$.

Every pure WZW vertex resolves into a tree of structure constants by expanding each Maurer–Cartan current into iterated Lie brackets. A resolved three-point diagram has five trivalent vertices and six internal edges. A self-loop vanishes. A double edge contracts to $C_A$ times the pairing, and a triangle to $\pm C_A/2$ times the three-leg bracket, from the adjoint trace and antisymmetry. If neither contraction applies, a vertex of degree three in the five-vertex simple graph has three mutually nonadjacent neighbors. The sixth edge count forces the remaining vertex to join all three. This gives $K_{2,3}$. Exchanging the two centers reverses the three structure constants at the external attachments and kills the tensor. Applying the same contractions to the smaller three-vertex graphs proves that the complete on-shell local three-point pole has the form

$$
\text{universal scalar}\ \times C_A^2 f_{abc}\,\omega(p_1,p_2)^3
$$

for a simple compact gauge algebra. With external permutations identified, the absolute coefficients of $C_A^2f_{abc}$ on the nine loopless connected trivalent graphs are $0,\,1/4,\,1/2,\,1$ with multiplicities $2,\,1,\,3,\,3$. This includes graphs with bridges. [Full integer color-tensor contractions for SU(2) and SU(3)](three_point_color.py)

An on-shell probe uses holomorphic components $(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)$ and independent antiholomorphic components $(0,1),(-1,0),(1,-1)$. All metric pairings vanish and $p_{1,\mathrm{hol}}\cdot p_{2,\mathrm{anti}}-p_{2,\mathrm{hol}}\cdot p_{1,\mathrm{anti}}=-2$. The cubic structure survives on this data. The following integrations evaluate its coefficient with an infrared regulator.

## Four contributing 1PI shapes before integration

If two external fields occur on the same original vertex, fix their total momentum and route it through the rest of the graph. The integrand has degree at most two in their relative momentum. The Kähler cube has degree three, so the local on-shell projection vanishes. With the three external vertices distinct, the internal graph has either one vertex of degree four or two of degree three. A one-particle-irreducible graph is therefore a figure-eight or a theta graph, with the remaining degree-two vertices subdividing its edges. Self-contractions are scaleless.

A figure-eight has at most three subdividing vertices. At least one circuit then has just one external cubic connected to the common junction by a double edge. Its WZ numerator is $\omega(p,k)$, where $p$ is that external momentum. Terms independent of $k$ at the other vertex integrate to zero. With one free $k^\mu$ the bubble produces $-p^2 B(p^2)\omega(p,\,\cdot\,)^\mu/[4(d-1)]$, where $B(p^2)$ is the scalar massless bubble. If both derivatives hit the bubble at the junction, a metric contraction gives zero by antisymmetry and a Kähler contraction gives a factor $(p^2)^2$. Thus every local figure-eight residue vanishes at $p^2=0$.

For a theta graph, count external cubic subdivisions on its three paths. With two external quartic junctions, the shape $(1,0,0)$ has a single WZ vertex, so every degree-six term contains a metric product of external momenta. With no external junction, $(1,1,1)$ has the zero color factor of $K_{2,3}$. The four remaining shapes are

| Junctions | Cubic subdivisions on paths |
|---|---|
| two internal cubics | $(3,0,0)$ or $(2,1,0)$ |
| one external quartic and one internal cubic | $(2,0,0)$ or $(1,1,0)$ |

## The three-point $(3,0,0)$ momentum integral

Use a real Euclidean Kähler form with orthogonal complex structure, continued by its algebraic contractions to $d=4-2\epsilon$. Strip color tensors, interaction coefficients, propagator constants and graph multiplicity. On the two parallel lines of momenta $k,q-k$ choose the cubic product $\omega(q,k)^2$. The tensor bubble above gives

$$
\int \frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{\omega(q,k)^2}{k^2(q-k)^2}
=-\frac{(q^2)^2}{4(d-1)}B(q^2),\qquad
B(q^2)=\frac{\Gamma(\epsilon)\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^2}{(4\pi)^{d/2}\Gamma(2-2\epsilon)}(q^2)^{-\epsilon}.
$$

The two adjacent $q^2$ propagators cancel. The cubic path numerator is

$$
\omega(p_1,q)\,\omega(p_2,q+p_1)\,\omega(p_3,q-p_3).
$$

For the remaining integral use denominators $(q+p_1)^2+m^2,(q-p_3)^2+m^2,(q^2+m^2)^z$ with Feynman parameters $x,y,t$ summing to one. The common $m>0$ regulates the infrared after isolating the tensor bubble. The shift of $q$ is $-xp_1+yp_3$. Fluctuation contractions in the numerator vanish because $\omega(p_i,\cdot)\cdot\omega(p_j,\cdot)=p_i\cdot p_j=0$. Writing $w=\omega(p_1,p_2)$, the three mean factors give $(-yw)(-tw)(-xw)$. Dirichlet integration gives the remaining momentum integral

$$
-\frac{w^3 (m^2)^{d/2-2-z}}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\,
\frac{z\Gamma(2+z-d/2)}{\Gamma(5+z)}.
$$

The one-loop bubble counterterm corresponds to $z=0$ and vanishes in this projection. For the graph $z=\epsilon$ and $\epsilon\Gamma(2\epsilon)\to1/2$. Multiplication by the bubble residue gives

$$
[\epsilon^{-1}]\ \text{oriented momentum integral}_{(3,0,0)}
=\frac{w^3}{576(4\pi)^4}.
$$

The common mass can also be inserted only in the two outer denominators. The shifted squared mass then equals $(x+y)m^2$; its power $-2\epsilon$ leaves the same finite limit of the convergent $xyt$ moment. [Feynman-parameter and independent angular-tensor replay](cubic_bubble.py)

## Background connection

In the inverse-field convention for (2.27), put $A_i=0$ and $A_{\bar j}=\sigma^{-1}\partial_{\bar j}\sigma$. A Cartan generator $H$ with nonzero adjoint action gives an exact local classical solution

$$
\sigma=\exp\!\big(H(|u_1|^2-|u_2|^2)\big),\qquad
\partial_1 A_{\bar1}+\partial_2 A_{\bar2}=0,\qquad
F_{1\bar1}=H,\quad F_{2\bar2}=-H .
$$

For a Lie-algebra-valued variation $\sigma^{-1}\delta\sigma$, the linearized equation has operator $\partial_1D_{\bar1}+\partial_2D_{\bar2}$ with $D_{\bar j}=\partial_{\bar j}+[A_{\bar j},\,\cdot\,]$. Its connection has nonzero curvature on this solution. Any finiteness proof using a flat fluctuation connection must identify that connection and justify its relation to this operator. Ketov §4 expressly leaves an all-order finiteness proof open. [Ketov, §§4–5](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(96)00784-8)

## Extension from three external fields to the local on-shell action

In (2.26) let $X=g^{-1}\partial g$, $Y=g^{-1}\bar\partial g$, $\eta=g^{-1}\delta g$. With the common factor $i/(8\pi^2)$ the kinetic variation gives $\int\omega\wedge\operatorname{tr}\eta(\partial Y-\bar\partial X)$. The Wess–Zumino variation gives $\int\omega\wedge\operatorname{tr}\eta(X\wedge Y+Y\wedge X)$ since $\delta\operatorname{tr}(g^{-1}dg)^3=3d\operatorname{tr}(\eta(g^{-1}dg)^2)$. Maurer–Cartan makes the sum $-2\int\omega\wedge\operatorname{tr}\eta\,\bar\partial X$. For $\sigma=g^{-1}$, $\partial A^{0,1}=g(\bar\partial X)g^{-1}$. Thus along $\sigma_t=\sigma e^{t\xi}$ the full variation is $-i/(4\pi^2)\int\omega\wedge\operatorname{tr}(\partial\xi\wedge A^{0,1}(t))$. Direct expansion gives

$$
A_{\bar i}(t)=A_{\bar i}
+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}t^m}{m!}
\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}^{m-1}D_{\bar i}\xi .
$$

Here $\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}Y=[\xi,Y]$. The order-one action term is the classical equation of motion after integration by parts. At every order $n\ge2$ the quantum vertex, divided by the common first-variation coefficient, is exactly

$$
\frac{(-1)^{n-2}}{n!}\int \operatorname{tr}
\big(\partial_i\xi\,\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}^{n-2}D_{\bar i}\xi\big).
$$

Replacing $\partial_i$ by $D_i$ extends all these vertices and the Gaussian Hessian to an arbitrary background connection. This agrees identically with the fluctuation expansion on $A_i=0$. Each vertex is an adjoint trace of covariant derivatives, including the pieces supplied by the Wess–Zumino term. Left multiplication by the background preserves the Haar measure; its exponential-coordinate Jacobian depends only on $\xi$, is invariant under conjugation, and its ultralocal vertices carry $\delta^{(d)}(0)=0$ in dimensional regularization.

Background gauge transformations conjugate the covariant scalar Hessian and its heat kernel at the two endpoints. Every contraction cancels the adjacent conjugations, even at a specified base point of the graph. Subdivergences can be subtracted by the same covariant coincident-point expansion. In radial gauge about that point, the connection satisfies $A_\mu(x)=\int_0^1 s\,x^\nu F_{\nu\mu}(sx)\,ds$, using coordinates centered there. Its Taylor coefficients are curvature tensors and their covariant derivatives. Thus the field-dependent local pole on this slice is represented by curvature polynomials. This derivation concerns flat space, a perturbative neighborhood of a constant group field and counterterms modulo total derivatives and local field redefinitions.

On a classical background the curvature is a primitive $(1,1)$ form. With the Kähler orientation it is anti-self-dual. At derivative degree six the possibilities are a cubic curvature, two curvatures with two derivatives, or a trace linear in derivatives of the curvature. The linear term vanishes on a simple Lie algebra. Products with a trace containing just one adjoint field also vanish. Integrating by parts reduces the quadratic sector to two first derivatives, up to commutators.

To account for the reduced $U(2)$ spacetime symmetry, write $S_+,S_-$ for the two two-dimensional complex rotation spin spaces; the primitive $(1,1)$ forms transform as $\operatorname{Sym}^2 S_-$ for the full $SU(2)_-$ subgroup of $U(2)$. Three spin-one factors have one invariant, their alternating product. Its color contraction reduces to $f_{abc}$. For two derivatives of curvature, the Bianchi identity and the Yang–Mills equation remove the spin-$1/2$ trace of $D F$ and leave $S_+\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^3 S_-$. The unique $SU(2)_-$ pairing of the spin-$3/2$ factors is antisymmetric. The trace pairs the two colors symmetrically, hence the $S_+$ indices must also pair antisymmetrically. This leaves the ordinary rotation-invariant contraction even with the Kähler form available. With $[D_\mu,D_\nu]=[F_{\mu\nu},\,\cdot\,]$, Bianchi and integration by parts give

$$
D^\rho D_\rho F_{\mu\nu}=2[F^\rho{}_\mu,F_{\rho\nu}],\qquad
\int\operatorname{tr}(D_\rho F_{\mu\nu}D^\rho F^{\mu\nu})
=-4\int\operatorname{tr}(F_\mu{}^\nu F_\nu{}^\rho F_\rho{}^\mu).
$$

The surviving invariant is detected by its cubic Taylor term. In the null probe above, the linearized mixed curvature of wave $i$ is the matrix $M_i=p_{i,\mathrm{hol}}p_{i,\mathrm{anti}}^T$ up to the common Fourier derivative sign. Direct multiplication gives $\operatorname{tr}(M_1[M_2,M_3])=2$. Terms proportional to the field equation have zero on-shell three-point matrix element: the linear equation kills each null external leg, and any invariant linear four-derivative field redefinition is a multiple of the squared Laplacian. External self-energy insertions contain a positive power of $p^2$ after amputation. Consequently the calculated vanishing three-point residue sets the coefficient of the unique on-shell action invariant to zero at two loops.

The result applies to the field-dependent, flat-space, pure WZW₄ logarithmic action counterterm in perturbation theory. The explicit off-shell sunset above and Costello's composite-operator OPE concern different quantities. Higher loops and the dynamics of a coupled closed field require separate calculations.

At $L$ loops the three-field null projection has degree $2L+2$ and hence is a multiple of $w^{L+1}$. Three external fields require an odd number of odd-valence vertices. Each such Wess–Zumino vertex contains one Kähler tensor, and every even-valence kinetic vertex uses only the metric. The amplitude changes sign under reversal of the complex structure. Thus every odd-loop three-field action pole vanishes. A three-loop action logarithm, if present, begins with at least four external fields.
