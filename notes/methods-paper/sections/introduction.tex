\section{Introduction and scope}
\label{intro:scope}

The electrical-network interpretation of a holomorphic Feynman graph
turns Schwinger parameters into resistances and external holomorphic
momenta into injected currents. It separates two questions. The first
is how to reconstruct the complete original current form after reducing
the network. The second is how to integrate the resulting rational
form. A local elimination can solve the first question exactly while
leaving nontrivial scalar periods in the second. Current insertions make
this distinction essential: an integrated bare kernel can vanish while
its complete first-current spinor is nonzero.

The starting current-area construction is that of
Li--Liu--Chang \cite{li2026}. We use the repository's ordered scalar
convention; its loop-dependent sign relative to that manuscript is
stated explicitly after \eqref{networks:density-cofactor}.
Wang's compactification and finiteness
results provide the holomorphic graph-integral setting
\cite{wang2025}. Gui--Wang--Williams already give an all-order cube
recursion for Type~I$'$ Laman weights
\cite[Theorem~4.13 and Remark~4.14]{gui2025}; their explicit examples
through three loops do not limit the scope of that recursion. The
network constructions below retain the original sources and currents
and cover product forests whose elimination does not remain within
that Type~I$'$ construction. We do not claim the original all-order
recursion, ultraviolet finiteness, or the higher-operation identities as
new results of this manuscript.

The second part of the paper concerns a fixed scalar Kirchhoff core.
Graph periods and their relative cohomology have a well-established
algebraic-geometric interpretation \cite{bloch2006,brown2015}; finiteness
of master-integral families is also a general theorem
\cite{smirnov2010}. Here we give a sectorwise proof adapted to the
particular problem of varying \emph{all positive integer pole orders}
and all convergent polynomial numerators on one fixed core. It keeps the
integration boundary and identifies a complete family of relative
derivative relations. Algebraic de Rham finiteness is an external
input \cite{grothendieck1966}; the sector convergence, boundary
presentation, pole-raising identities and wheel constructions are
proved explicitly in the text.

\subsection{Results and their distinct meanings}

The paper establishes the following statements.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Sourced leaf elimination is an exact identity of networks with
all original current maps retained. Differentiation includes every
source-transfer coefficient. For the product-forest class considered
here, each eliminated vertex has at most three remaining neighbours.
\item On compact spanning-tree cubes, every finite polynomial in
the original currents has an explicit rational series representation.
Each series coefficient and a closed upper bound on the entire tail
are rational calculations. This is an exact infinite-series answer;
a finite truncation remains accompanied by its remainder.
\item Theorem~\ref{periods:finite-span} proves finite rational period
span and eventual stabilization for a fixed normalized core at
positive integer denominator powers. It does not compute a general
stabilization threshold or a minimal numerical period basis.
\item Theorem~\ref{periods:wheel-span} computes the full stable scalar
span for every wheel, with the explicit sufficient threshold
$q\ge\max(3,n-1)$. No arithmetic independence of real zeta values is
assumed.
\item Section~\ref{currents:family} gives the complete source dependence
of the bare kernel and every first-current spinor of one physical
quartic five-loop forest. The formulas retain all source directions
and extend polynomially to degenerate root-source frames.
\end{enumerate}

These statements have different scopes. A complete sourced network
reduction is not, by itself, a finite zeta evaluation. A finite scalar
period space need not be a field or an algebra, and its dimension need
not equal the number of displayed zeta generators. A graph's geometric
kernel is not the full Wick, colour and coupling sum of an interacting
observable. We attach none of those additional claims to the results
below.

\subsection{Normalization and relation to dimensions}

All explicitly computed forest kernels use two holomorphic directions
and no topological direction, denoted $H=2,T=0$. The heat-kernel
normalization is removed once and the remaining loop sign is fixed by
\eqref{networks:area-definition}--\eqref{networks:density-cofactor};
ordered edge orientations and the projective sign are retained.
An inserted original current is the negative first derivative with
respect to the original edge displacement. A scalar core period uses
the positive projective measure, without dividing by edge gamma
factors. Section~\ref{periods:normalization} states that scalar
normalization before any graph transformation.

The fixed-core theorem is not confined to $H=2$. It applies to any
dimension or finite insertion order that reduces, after its stated
normalization, to a rational polynomial numerator over a positive
integer power of the same Kirchhoff polynomial. It does not cover
noninteger analytic regularization, unrestricted logarithmic
insertions, or additional topological contact distributions merely
by changing a dimension label.
